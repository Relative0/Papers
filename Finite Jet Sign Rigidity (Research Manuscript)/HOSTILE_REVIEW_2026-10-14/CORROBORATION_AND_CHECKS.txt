Direct mathematical checks

1. Sections 3-6: the polynomial weak-sign lemma needs nonzero P, but permits
interior zeros. A relatively open facet cannot be contained in the residual
polynomial's zero set; hence a generic transverse pair really recovers the
maximal factor multiplicity modulo two. Local Taylor polynomials need only
inherit weak signs. The overlapping-ball argument propagates actual crossing
ratios, avoiding any assumption of constant Taylor order. Uniform finite-order
rigidity includes N=0; the smooth statement correctly requires a single flat
point and is not deduced by interchanging its quantifiers. Distinct hyperplanes
each have a generic facet, so the 2^(m+1) parity restrictions are distinct even
though the chamber sign vectors do not generally form a Boolean cube.

2. Sections 5-6: squaring a nonzero cutoff and integrating from its left support
endpoint gives a selector positive at EVERY positive argument. Possible gaps
in the cutoff's support do not break the construction. Log convexity and
monotonicity supply the finite-product and primitive estimates used here; no
unmentioned derivative-stability assumption enters. On a chamber, exactly one
summand in the explicit formula survives, so cancellation cannot occur.

3. Proposition 7.1, source lines 509-528, PDF p. 12: k=0 follows from continuity
and F(0)=0. For k>=1, D^k F(0)=0 and its Holder bound give the ray-integral
remainder. Its beta integral is exactly
Gamma(alpha+1)/Gamma(k+alpha+1), including alpha=1. The Euclidean distance of
delta*s is sqrt(n)*delta, and the ball-radius condition is stated.

4. Remark 7.2, source lines 530-540, PDF p. 12: the order-j Taylor remainder
cancels j! in the derivative budget. j=0 supplies the amplitude bound. A
coordinate-partial estimate converts to the multilinear operator estimate via
the product of l1 norms of unit vectors, bounded by n^(j/2). Thus replacing B
by B*sqrt(n) is sufficient. The constants are fixed on the relevant ball or
compact subball. The Gevrey upper estimate follows by optimizing the integer
order; the manuscript does not claim a general matching cutoff theorem.

5. Theorem 7.3(i), source line 576, PDF p. 13: nonmultilinear monomials cause no
problem in the parity-correlation argument. A monomial of total degree below
|S| misses at least one parity variable, so its product with parity averages
to zero over the Boolean vertices. Strict sign agreement would instead make
that average positive. Constant laws correctly have degree zero.

6. Theorem 7.3(ii), source lines 578-608, PDF pp. 13-14: the dyadic construction
has an everywhere positive denominator because its two nonnegative summands
have disjoint real roots. A nearest lower dyadic node supplies the stated
1/3 factor. Clearing the n positive denominators costs at most the stated
total degree. The binary-face reduction is valid over F2. Non-affine binary
laws are exactly the eight input/output-flipped copies of AND. For the lower
bound, odd N avoids ties, and N<=1/delta ensures that the substituted majority
averages stay in the guarded square. These substitutions do not increase
degree. Sherstov's theorem supplies the claimed logarithmic lower bound.

7. Theorem 7.3(iii), source lines 610-623, PDF p. 14: a changing Boolean edge
fixes a univariate slice with a 2*gamma change across an interval of length
2*delta. The whole-cube norm bound is essential for Bernstein's inequality.
The clipped ramp composed with cos is globally periodic and 1/delta-Lipschitz.
Symmetrizing a trigonometric Jackson approximant gives a cosine polynomial;
Chebyshev conversion preserves degree. Dividing by 1+eta supplies the claimed
whole-interval bound, and the expectation extension supplies the final margin.
The degree may depend on fixed n and gamma. No growing-arity claim is inferred.

8. Remark 7.4: gamma=1 is impossible for a nonconstant law, since equality in
the amplitude bound makes the polynomial constant on an open guarded box.
Sign-preserving denominator clearing does not preserve a normalized fixed
margin. As an explicit stress check, in the manuscript's binary construction
with L=1 and delta=2^(-m), the norm is q(1)^2 and its normalized corner magnitude
is 2^(-2*m*(m+1)). For m=8 this is about 4.48e-44. This supports the separation
between the two resource models; it does not challenge either degree theorem.

9. Sections 8.3-8.4, source lines 664-680, PDF pp. 15-16: the R-conjunction
has smooth angular part away from zero and positive homogeneity k+1. Its kth
derivative is degree-one homogeneous with bounded derivative away from zero;
splitting close and widely separated pairs gives the Lipschitz extension.
If F_k were C^(k+1), homogeneity would make it a homogeneous polynomial,
contradicting its non-affine whole-quadrant signs. The diagonal value gives
the claimed sharp exponent for alpha=1. The regularized root is analytic
because its radicand is strictly positive. Its guarded positive-quadrant
minimum is (2-sqrt(3))*delta, whereas its value at the origin is -delta.
The manuscript therefore respects the common-corner hypothesis.
