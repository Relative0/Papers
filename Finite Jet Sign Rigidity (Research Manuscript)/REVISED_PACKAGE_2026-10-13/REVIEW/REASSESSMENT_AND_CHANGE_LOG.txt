DECISION

Integrate into Finite Jet. The current branch does not make a sufficiently strong
case for a second research manuscript, even a short technical note. This is a
judgment about contribution and portfolio separation, not a claim that a modest
expository article would be unpublishable.

The strongest standalone candidate would have been a note on polynomial signs
away from coordinate walls. It has a coherent statement: at fixed arity, parity
has constant degree, every non-affine law has logarithmic degree in inverse gap,
and every nonconstant law has reciprocal-gap degree under a whole-cube amplitude
bound and fixed margin. That is useful enough to include as an explicit theorem.
Its ingredients, however, are close to established results: the difficult lower
bound is Sherstov's conjunction-of-majorities theorem; the upper bound uses a
standard rational-approximation construction; the normalized result is a short
Bernstein/Jackson argument. The reduction to a non-affine two-face supplies an
economical synthesis, but it does not establish a new lower-bound technique.

I reconsidered the lower publication bar of an expository or technical article.
Synthesis can be publishable, and using classical inputs alone is not grounds
for rejection. Here, the existing Finite Jet manuscript is already positioned as
a focused synthesis note. Adding four pages gives that paper a stronger account
of what changes when correctness is required only away from the walls, without
requiring a second article to repeat its framework and novelty qualifications.

The other candidates are weaker as independent contributions. Analytic-model
impossibility is a direct corollary under the full independent-coordinate domain
assumption; Taylor corner estimates are standard consequences of zero jets; the
associated-function bound is standard ultradifferentiable analysis; classical
R-functions already supply exact finite-differentiability alternatives.

INFORMAL PROOF AUDIT OF THE ADDITIONS

Corner estimate: if any derivative through order k survived, its least order
would trigger the existing finite-order theorem. The one-dimensional ray
remainder then gives the stated constant; k=0 is handled directly. Operator
norms are specified, so no unspoken coordinate-to-operator identification occurs.

Associated function: flatness permits a remainder of every order j, cancelling
j! against the budget. The j=0 case uses the amplitude bound. The weight is M
with factorial outside, consistently with the original paper. Matching optimal
cutoffs and claims about parameter-gradient optimization were deliberately omitted.

Rational upper bound: m is the ceiling of log2(1/delta), ensuring the dyadic
interval contains the entire guarded interval. Even powers make numerator inputs
nonnegative; their zero sets are disjoint, so q is everywhere positive. One
dyadic factor gives the needed ratio bound. The multilinear expectation is
bounded on the whole cube, and the union bound supplies a uniform sign margin
before denominator clearing. Total polynomial degree is at most 2Ln(m+1).

Non-affine lower bound: vanishing of all two-face second differences is equivalent
to an affine Boolean law. All eight non-affine binary laws reduce to AND by
input/output flips. Odd N ensures every normalized sum has magnitude at least
1/N; taking N <= 1/delta maps an actual finite Boolean cube into the guarded
domain. Polynomial substitution cannot increase degree. The source's true=-1
convention is explicitly reconciled. The finite-set theorem is never applied
directly to an infinite interval.

Normalized bounds: the changing Boolean edge exists for every nonconstant law,
including parity. The amplitude bound is on the whole cube, so the univariate
Bernstein inequality applies between the endpoints +/-delta. For the upper
bound, symmetrized Jackson approximation, rescaling, and the expectation
extension keep the polynomial bounded globally. The strict gamma<1 condition is
essential. No conclusion about sign-only polynomial margin is smuggled in.

R-functions: positive homogeneity and a smooth angular factor yield the C^{k,1}
extension with zero k-jet. C^{k+1} would force equality with a homogeneous Taylor
polynomial, contradicting non-affine exact signs. The rounded analytic example
is increasing in both positive inputs, and either negative input bounds the
score above by -delta. It does not satisfy the whole-orthant specification.

No gap was found in this informal audit. It is neither an independent adversarial
referee pass nor a machine-checked mathematical proof.
