Finite-Jet Sign Rigidity on Hyperplane Arrangements
and Denjoy–Carleman Realization
Brian Theory
27 September 2026
Abstract
LetA be a finite real affine hyperplane arrangement. We study which{±1}-valued chamber
labelings can occur as the sign of a function with prescribed regularity. A first nonzero
homogeneous Taylor term inherits the chamber signs weakly, and factor multiplicities force
a constant crossing parity. This gives a short arrangement-level proof of the known smooth
signature-flatness obstruction and, in finite regularity, explicitC d orthant andC N arrangement
rigidity statements.
For normalized compactwise Roumieu Denjoy–Carleman classesE {M }, the Denjoy–Carleman
criterion yields an exact realization dichotomy. In the quasianalytic case the realizable chamber
laws are precisely the signed parity laws; in the non-quasianalytic case every chamber labeling
is realized by explicit selector formulas. Thus an arrangement ofm distinct hyperplanes
with r chambers admits exactly2m+1 and 2r realizable sign laws in the two regimes. The
smooth obstruction and positive-flat existence mechanisms are classical; the contribution is the
finite-order formulation, self-contained affine-arrangement proof, and unified exact realization
classification.

Keywords. finite jets; hyperplane arrangements; Denjoy–Carleman classes; quasianalyticity; sign
distributions; chamber functions.
1 Introduction
A Boolean truth table is finite. Requiring the same truth value throughout an entire orthant of
Rn is not. This distinction becomes sharp when the Boolean value is extracted from the sign of a
smooth real-valued function.
For example, the polynomial
F(x,y ) =xy
is positive in the first and third quadrants and negative in the other two. With positive interpreted
as 1 and negative as0, it realizes equivalence (XNOR), an affine Boolean function overF2. By
contrast, the AND sign pattern is positive only in the positive-positive quadrant and negative in
the other three. The nonsmooth functionmin(x,y ) has exactly that sign pattern, but the results
below show that anysmooth realization of AND on all four quadrants must be flat at the origin.
In a quasianalytic class this is impossible for a nonzero function; in a non-quasianalytic class it is
possible by using flat one-sided selectors.
The first purpose of this paper is to isolate the local mechanism behind that phenomenon. It is
not coordinate division, resolution of singularities, or a special feature of one ultradifferentiable
class. It is the first nonzero homogeneous Taylor term. If a smooth function has a nonzero finite
jet at the common corner, its leading polynomial already inherits the orthant signs in a weak

The second purpose is to carry that mechanism to a finite affine hyperplane arrangement. If
a smooth function is nonzero off the arrangement and has no flat point on it, the local leading

Thus neither the smooth obstruction nor the leading-term idea is claimed as new.

functions [14, 13, 15]. Stronger division and monomialization frameworks are also available [11, 9,
10], but they are not needed for the elementary proofs below.
The note contributes three things in one framework: explicit finite-order rigidity statements; a
self-contained leading-jet proof for arbitrary finite affine arrangements; and exact quasianalytic/non-
quasianalytic realization classifications with constructive formulas and chamber counts. The
purpose is therefore not historical firstness, but a compact finite-jet formulation and an exact
synthesis across regularity regimes.

theorem.

7.1 Real zero support is weaker than a normal-crossing divisor

Indeed,

This is why the arrangement theorem above is stated and proved directly at the sign level.
Resolution and monomialization results involve stronger divisor hypotheses and/or transformations
of source and target; they remain relevant background, not a replacement for the present argument.

7.3 Smoothness alone does not imply rigidity
The selector construction in(9) givesC∞realizations of every Boolean law in the non-quasianalytic

construction is consistent with exactly that requirement. The mathematical boundary is therefore
not “smooth versus nonsmooth,” but rather the presence or absence of a nonzero finite jet at the
relevant common boundary.

7.4 Signatures and chamber functions
The factor-parity mechanism in Lemma 3.1 is classical antecedent material. Broglia–Tognoli’s
Proposition 2.4 records how parity of maximal factor multiplicities controls the induced signa-
ture [12, Proposition 2.4], while the subsequent signature and sign-distribution literature studies
broader realization and approximation questions [5, 1, 2, 4, 3].
A distinct combinatorial tradition studies all functions that are constant on arrangement
chambers. Varchenko and Gelfand introduced the algebra generated by hyperplane Heaviside
functions [7]; see also [8, 6]. The present analytic question is narrower: regularity constraints
select a small family of{±1}-valued chamber functions in the quasianalytic regime, while the
non-quasianalytic regime permits them all.
7.5 Established machinery versus the sign classification
Stronger division, quasianalytic local-ring, resolution, and monomialization frameworks are avail-
able [15, 11, 9, 10]. The proofs here deliberately avoid that machinery: the rigidity input is only the
first nonzero Taylor polynomial, and the realization input is only the classical Denjoy–Carleman
criterion together with cutoffs or positive-flat functions. This is the sense in which the note is
self-contained without claiming foundational priority.
8 Conclusion
A first nonzero Taylor term provides a compact bridge from local regularity to global chamber signs.
In finite order it yields explicitCd orthant andCN arrangement obstructions; in the smooth setting
the same mechanism gives an elementary proof of the established signature-flatness consequence
for affine arrangements. Combined with the Denjoy–Carleman criterion, the argument gives an
exact realization classification: form distinct hyperplanes withr chambers, the full normalized
Roumieu class realizes exactly2m+1 signed parity laws in the quasianalytic case and all2r chamber
labelings in the non-quasianalytic case.
The note’s contribution is therefore the finite-order formulation, the direct affine-arrangement
proof, and the explicit synthesis of rigidity, construction, and exact counting in one normalization.
The underlying smooth obstruction and positive-flat existence theory are classical and are used
here as historical and technical context rather than as claims of first discovery.
