Abstract
Probabilistic Approximate Logic (PALO) combines symbolic domain theories with con-
tinuous, learnable models. The correspondence-matrix portfolio develops exact Boolean
representations, a bounded compiler, certified extraction of Boolean operators from continu-
ous scores, regularity restrictions on sign realizations, and separate finite-ring support and
process theories. These projects share several interfaces, but they do not share one semantics.
The closest practical connection is guarded extraction and validation of discrete behavior
from a PALO-style learned model. The closest algebraic connection is that real-valued
contraction of a binary correspondence matrix reproduces PALO’s primitive soft connective
formulas under a product-distribution interpretation. This report derives that connection,
makes its dependence assumptions explicit, and shows why exact Boolean rewrites need
not preserve a soft learning objective. Finite-jet rigidity supplies a conditional expressivity
boundary for uniform sign specifications. The chain-ring papers provide more distant lessons
about support, composition, and global consistency.
The report develops benefits in both directions and a staged research agenda. It separates
statements in the supplied manuscripts, elementary consequences established here, and
proposed work. It reports no new learning benchmark, integrated implementation, historical-
priority determination, or independent certification of the source manuscripts.
Scope: seven primary documents, comprising PALO and six portfolio manuscripts. Prepared from the
supplied local versions; this is a comparative research report, not a revision of those manuscripts.

1 Executive assessment
The projects overlap most strongly at the boundary between continuous numerical behavior and
exact logical meaning. PALO asks how uncertain domain knowledge can guide the construction
of useful learned models. The CM/LM calculus asks how exact Boolean functions, symbolic
formulas, and numerical operator representations can be related coherently. Guarded dispatch
asks when a continuous model determines a particular Boolean operator, and what information
certifies that determination. These are complementary questions.

Three conclusions organize the report.
1. There is an exact, small algebraic bridge.For a binary CMC, the real contraction
[p, 1−p]C[q, 1−q]T gives the same primitive formulas used by PALO for conjunction,
disjunction, implication, and equivalence. The truth table alone does not determine the
recursive soft evaluation of every classically equivalent formula.
2. Certification is the most natural added capability.Guarded dispatch can specify
when thresholded observations identify an exact operator on a declared region. Extending
this to PALO would require a suitable model family, a concrete abstraction, and sound
certificates; the existing note is not a general neural verifier.
3. The benefits depend on preserving semantic distinctions.A Boolean identity, a
probability identity, a soft numerical identity, and a support identity are different claims.
The portfolio’s strongest contribution to a PALO-style architecture may be making these
contracts explicit.
PALO can reciprocally give the portfolio a motivated application: reasoning with imperfect
observations, testing theories on held-out data, learning model parameters, and measuring when
exact symbolic structure improves useful predictions. It does not by itself prove that CMs are
faster, that guards are cheap, or that a particular regularity obstruction explains training failure.

3 PALO’s architecture and the relevant semantic distinctions
3.1 What PALO is trying to achieve
PALO’s starting point is that domain knowledge can partly substitute for data or improve
learning from available data. Constants, functions, and predicates receive interpretations in
parameterized continuous model classes. Predicates produce values in[0, 1]; their interpretations
can involve neural networks, prescribed functions, or semantic attachments. A theory supplies
constraints and an objective for model construction [1, Sections 1, 3.1–3.4].
The formal framework describes a Bayesian model-space perspective, including learnable theory
weights as well as model parameters. The reported prototype approximates posterior-mode
behavior using repeated optimization and model synthesis. The manuscript explicitly says that
the full posterior-distribution semantics had not been implemented in that prototype [1, p. 28,
note 29]. Consequently, an ensemble of reported model-synthesis runs should not automatically
be called exact posterior sampling.
3.2 Four interpretations that should remain distinct
PALO’s semantics table distinguishes approximate probability evaluation, lower/upper proba-
bility evaluation, abstract classical reasoning, and a concrete classical interpretation based on
crispification [1, Sections 3.5–3.8, pp. 23–27]. For the primitive binary connectives, with inputs
p,q∈[0, 1], its approximate formulas include
¬p = 1−p, p ∧q =pq, (1)
p∨q =p +q−pq, p ⇒q = 1−p +pq,
p⇔q = 1−p−q + 2pq.
The equivalence formula is specified directly; replacing it with any classically equivalent syntax
need not preserve this soft value.
For a finite nonempty batch of valuesv1,...,vN, the displayed base quantifier semantics uses the
maximum for existence, the geometric mean for universal quantification, and the arithmetic mean
for the many/mean quantifier. Further sampling over batches forms the mean interpretation.
These choices are essential to PALO, rather than incidental implementation details [1, Sections
3.2–3.3].
In particular, a high soft universal value is not a proof that all instances exceed a threshold.
If one of 100 values is0.1 and the others are1, their geometric mean is0.11/100≈0.977. The
poorly satisfied instance remains. A Boolean certificate therefore cannot be inferred from a high
aggregate score alone.
3.3 Symbolic computation is already part of PALO
LIME first uses Maude for symbolic processing and then compiles the result into numerical
computations. Its reported symbolic operations include formula simplification and equational
reduction; the multisets-and-sums example illustrates symbolic definitions feeding numerical
evaluation [1, Section 4, pp. 27–30 and 41–43]. A CM contribution would need to augment or
specialize this existing layer.
The paper also explicitly discusses the fact that adding classically implied axioms can change
approximate inference. Classically equivalent theories need not have the same soft objective, and
additional derived knowledge can help until its cost or redundancy becomes counterproductive [1,

4.3 Which parts of the CM architecture survive
Reindexing is compatible with real expectation: swapping input roles transposesC, and comple-
menting the first input corresponds to a row swap together withp↦→1−p. More generally, a
permutation of assignment coordinates preserves the pairing if the distribution coordinates are
permuted consistently. Frame metadata therefore remains useful.
Other identities do not survive unchanged. The Boolean frame matrix
SX =

¬X X
)
is involutory in the Boolean calculus. Substituting a probabilityp and using real arithmetic gives
determinant 2p−1, so the resulting matrix is singular atp = 1/2 and is not generally involutory.
The LM normal-form theorem is consequently not a ready-made theorem about soft probability
frames.
The portfolio’s numeric CMs, formula-valued LMs, and PALO neural-tensor parameter arrays
also have different meanings. Similar shapes or notation do not identify them. An integration
should declare the role of each tensor before combining it with another.
4.4 Benefits in both directions
Proposed extension.For PALO, the CM representation could provide a uniform, inspectable
library of small connective kernels with explicit input order and polarity. It could support exact
corner tests, signed-frame normalization, and comparisons between alternative soft interpretations
of the same Boolean connective.
For CM/LM, PALO supplies a concrete reason to study evaluations beyond Boolean valuations:
probabilities, uncertainty bounds, learned predicate values, and data-dependent objective func-
tions. Such an extension should retain the existing Boolean calculus as a distinct layer and
establish new compatibility laws only where they hold.

5.3 Why operator fusion is delicate
The Boolean CM law applies an outer connective pointwise to aligned truth tables. For Boolean
functions f,g of the same assignmentZ, exact expectation gives
E[f(Z)g(Z)] = E[f(Z)]E[g(Z)] + Cov(f(Z),g (Z)).
Applying a product soft AND to the separately evaluated outputs drops the covariance. With
g =f, f(Z)2 =f(Z), so the fused Boolean table has expectationE[f(Z)], while the unfused
product is its square. This is the precise reason the Boolean superposition/coherence theorem is
not automatically a soft-evaluation theorem.

A sufficient condition for product composition is independence of the output events. A useful
special case is that the operands depend on disjoint sets of independent underlying Boolean vari-
ables. Disjoint syntax alone is insufficient if the variables are dependent; conversely, some special
functions can have independent outputs even with overlapping dependencies. An implementation
should use the condition it can establish, rather than treating a heuristic as a proof.
5.4 A proposed dependence-aware extension
Proposed extension. A small Boolean block could carry a truth tensor together with an
explicitly declared joint distribution or a feasible set of distributions. Evaluation would use
expectation or optimization over that set. This would add exact local dependence handling and
could tighten uncertainty information relative to a purely product-based evaluation.
The costs are substantive: joint states grow with arity, estimated correlations can be unreli-
able, and overlapping blocks need consistency constraints. Changing from PALO’s recursive
approximation to block expectation changes the semantics and training objective. It should be
exposed as a new mode with separate evaluation, rather than installed as a supposedly invisible
optimization.
PALO benefits from a precise account of what dependence information a block consumes. The
portfolio benefits from a meaningful probabilistic interpretation of operator coefficients and a
way to identify which connective is sensitive to missing dependence. Neither direction supplies a
general efficient probabilistic inference algorithm.
6 The CM compiler as a possible PALO component
6.1 Where it could fit
Source result. The compiler manuscript normalizes signed/swapped binary frames, folds
compatible operators, distinguishes structural from tabulated and hybrid derivations, and falls
back to ordinary processing when its selected strategy does not succeed [3, Sections 3–5]. Its
legality and provenance rules are potentially useful in a PALO compiler interface.
There are three materially different insertion points:
1. Exact classical preparation.Normalize or simplify a Boolean fragment before selecting
its soft realization. Classical meaning is preserved, while a changed soft objective must be
recorded and evaluated as such.
2. Soft execution. Rewrite an already chosen numerical graph only under identities valid in
that soft semantics, including any dependence assumptions. A Boolean truth-table check
alone is insufficient.
3. Concrete classical validation.Once predicate interpretations have been crispified under
a declared policy, use Boolean transformations to evaluate admitted propositional fragments
exactly. Quantifier, sort, and grounding behavior remains separately specified.
The third point is the cleanest initial use because its Boolean contract is explicit. The first is
scientifically interesting because reformulations may improve learning, but it requires accuracy
and optimization experiments. The second demands the strongest semantic discipline.

6.2 A proposed transformation contract
A transformed expression should retain its operand frame, free-variable bindings, connective
interpretation, arithmetic domain, provenance, and zero/threshold policy where relevant. Any
claimed benefit should state what is preserved: Boolean corner values, a full soft function, a
joint-distribution expectation, or only a set of downstream certified labels.
Common-subexpression reuse can preserve numerical semantics if it merely shares the evaluation
of the same deterministic subexpression. It must not silently replace a repeated occurrence’s
role in the enclosing soft formula. For example, computingp once and multiplying that value by
itself preservesp2; simplifying the two occurrences to one factor does not.
6.3 Performance evidence and suitable baselines
Source result. The supplied compiler manuscript reports a generic-optimizer tie for some
local reductions and negative results for frozen comparisons. In its synthetic pair-token study
the median direct-packed/CM production-time ratio is0.248, so the direct producer is faster
under that contract. The descriptive natural-circuit timing result is also negative for the CM
producer [3, Section 8].
These results do not rule out a useful role in another workload, but they rule out treating the
representation itself as evidence of acceleration. A PALO integration should compare against
ordinary truth tables, generic small-function rewriting, and the existing symbolic/numerical
baseline. It should charge recognition, guards, data transfer, compilation, graph construction,
training, and repeated evaluation separately. Fewer logical operations or sign probes need not
mean lower wall time.
Proposed extension.PALO would supply application-driven tests of whether typed local folding
reduces repeated work. In return, the compiler’s explicit fallback and provenance boundaries
could make PALO transformations easier to audit. A convincing outcome might be improved
reliability or traceability even if the CM notation provides no speed advantage.

7.2 Thresholding a formula differs from evaluating thresholded atoms
Forτ= 1/2, lets(P ) = s(Q) = 0.6. Thresholding the soft conjunction givescrisp(0.36) = 0.
Evaluating the Boolean conjunction of the crisp atoms gives1∧1 = 1. The two procedures do
not commute.
PALO’s concrete classical semantics inserts crispification into its semantic interpretation; it should
not be summarized as merely thresholding the final answer of an arbitrary soft computation [1,
Section 3.7]. A dispatcher must therefore identify its intended target: the thresholded original
soft score, the concrete classical interpretation, or another explicitly defined operator.
At equality, PALO’s displayed crispification uses the conventioncrispτ(v) = 1 for v≥τ. The
guard note’s strict threshold is undefined at zero. A combined interface can retain a boundary
flag and apply the PALO tie convention for ordinary evaluation, but a tie must not be described
as evidence of a strict sign margin.
7.3 Representative inputs are not automatically full predicate cells
The note’s collecting semantics is especially relevant to interpretability. LetD be a concrete
input domain andP :D→{0, 1}n a predicate abstraction. For a scoreg, define
Sα={sgn0g(x) :x∈D, P(x) =α}.
The empty set is an unreachable cell;{−1}and{+1}are exact strict outputs; a set containing
zero records a tie; a set containing both signs records actual variation. An incomplete solver’s
uncertainty is a separate epistemic status [4, Section 4.3].
Consequently, evaluating one representative from each apparent Boolean input cell does not
establish an operator valid on the full domain. If only some cells are reachable, a reachability
mask or partial operator is appropriate. A total CM/LM output requires an explicit completion
policy or a proof of the full-cube conditions.
7.4 Margins provide a modest robustness certificate
Proposition 7.1(Local stability from score margins). Suppose the finite score family satisfies
|gα(λ0)|≥γ >0. Suppose each score isL-Lipschitz on a neighborhood in a fixed norm. IfL> 0,
then every point in that neighborhood with∥λ−λ0∥<γ/Lhas the same strict-sign operator as
λ0.
Proof. The score change is strictly less thanγ, so no score can reach or cross zero. Apply this to
every coordinate. IfL = 0, the scores are constant on the region to which the bound applies.
This elementary bound requires a valid uniform Lipschitz bound, not merely a gradient measured
at one point. A certificate of stability in parameter space is also different from one in input space.
More precise guards may be much larger than the conservative ball certified by this argument.

Proposed extension. PALO could gain an optional certified Boolean interface for selected

8 Finite-jet rigidity: a conditional boundary on exact realization
8.1 What the theorem requires
Source result.The finite-jet manuscript studies functions nonzero throughout the open orthants
of a neighborhood of the common corner. If such a smooth function has any nonzero derivative at
the corner, its sign law is affine parity. Thus a smooth realization of a non-affine Boolean orthant
law must be flat at the corner. An explicit finite-order version only needs the corresponding
finite differentiability and a first nonzero Taylor term [5, Theorems 4.1 and 4.3].
For suitable normalized Denjoy–Carleman classes, quasianalyticity rules out nonzero flat germs
and restricts the realizable laws to signed parity. On the non-quasianalytic side, selector
constructions realize all chamber labelings. For an arrangement ofm distinct hyperplanes withr
chambers, the manuscript gives counts2m+1 and 2r in the respective regimes [5, Sections 5–6].
These source theorems are not re-certified here.
8.2 The precise connection to smooth learned predicates
Suppose a proposed learned scoreF must return a strict Boolean label depending only on signs
of its real input coordinates, uniformly across magnitudes arbitrarily near their common zero.
The finite-jet theorem then constrains the score’s regularity. A nonflat smooth score cannot
realize whole-orthant AND under these assumptions; parity-type laws such as XNOR remain
possible.
PALO’s illustrated predicate families include finite compositions of affine or polynomial operations
with sigmoid and hyperbolic tangent. For finite real parameters, such compositions are real
analytic; subtracting a fixed threshold retains analyticity. Therefore, if an individual score
from such a family were required to satisfy the whole-orthant specification just described, the
analytic special case of the rigidity result would apply. This is a conditional deduction about the
chosen score and domain, not a theorem about all PALO expressions: existential maxima, other
attachments, and nonsmooth components can change the regularity assumptions.
8.3 Why this is not a prohibition on learning AND
Three specifications must be distinguished:
1. matching a Boolean truth table at a finite collection of representative points;
2. achieving approximate predictive or soft-logical performance on a distribution;
3. having the exact prescribed sign everywhere in every local orthant around a common
corner.
The third is much stronger. The finite-jet theorem addresses it, whereas PALO’s main learning
objectives usually concern the second, and a representative truth-table extraction may establish
only the first.
The dispatch scorex +y +xy demonstrates the distinction. Ata =b = 1/2, its four signed-
representative values are5/4,−1/4,−1/4,−3/4, which encode AND. Ata = 2,b = 1/2, they
encode projection onto the first input. The polynomial therefore does not implement one AND
law across all magnitudes or throughout the four orthants, so it does not contradict rigidity.

8.4 How each project could extend the other
Proposed extension.The theorem could serve as a specification audit before attempting a
certified learning task. If an exact uniform rule is incompatible with the selected analytic score
class, one could change the domain, weaken the requirement to a margin-separated subset, use
guarded regions, or consider a suitable nonsmooth or flat construction. Each choice changes the
assumptions and must be documented.
PALO could provide experiments comparing these alternatives and measuring their learning
behavior. Flat smooth realizations have vanishing derivatives at the corner, suggesting a possible
optimization challenge near that point. The manuscript proves flatness, not a general vanishing-
gradient training theorem, sample-complexity bound, or failure of every learning algorithm.
Themostinformativestudywouldcompareexactrealizabilityandempiricallearnabilityseparately.
A model can fit finite data well while violating a whole-orthant requirement; another can have
an exact realization but be difficult to train. Connecting those two facts requires new analysis or
controlled experiments.
9 Chain-ring contextuality and process semantics
9.1 A weaker connection that should remain separate
The chain-ring contextuality manuscript classifies support behavior of bipartite matrices using
Smith data under a specified measurement contract. Withr nonzero Smith factors and leading
multiplicityh, its classes are local forr = 1, logically but not strongly contextual forr≥2,h = 1,
and strongly contextual forh≥2 [6, Abstract and Section 3].
These are possible/impossible outcome statements. PALO’s soft truth values, learned weights,
and approximate probabilities are different objects. The contextuality paper supplies no Born
rule or probability model, and neither the CM/LM pairing nor the ring amplitudes should be
treated as neural confidence scores.
9.2 Local information and global consistency
Derived connection.A possible comparison concerns local constraints that cannot be assembled
into one global assignment. In a strongly contextual support scenario, no deterministic global
assignment is compatible with all contextual supports. An ordinary probability distribution
over such compatible assignments therefore cannot exist: there are no assignments to support it.
In merely logical contextuality, some supported local events cannot be so extended, a weaker
obstruction.
This should be distinguished from ordinary statistical dependence. Correlated variables may
have a perfectly valid global joint distribution. Contextuality is not just large covariance or a
failure of a product approximation.
Proposed extension. PALO could be used experimentally to fit separate context-indexed
predicates to such finite support constraints and to compare soft satisfaction with exact global
feasibility checks. Strong soft scores would not establish a global assignment. The existing
support classification could provide small test cases for that distinction, but a detailed encoding,
baseline solver, and success criterion would be needed. This is lower priority than guarded
extraction because it is further from PALO’s demonstrated applications.

9.3 Composition contracts and the nilpotent obstruction
Source result.The process companion studiesA = F2[u]/(u4). Declaring every nonzero scalar
possible and composing independent scalar events by multiplication fails becauseu3 and u are
nonzero butu3u = 0. Primitive preparations do not repair selective conditioning. A closed
binary-modal completion and a coarse realization of the original support tables are developed
instead [7, Sections 2–4].
This obstruction is not PALO’s product-independence approximation in disguise. The ring
example is a failure of a proposed possibility predicate to preserve multiplication; PALO’s issue
is that probabilities of dependent events are not generally products of marginals. The common
lesson is to specify what composition means and what an interpretation preserves.
The companion also separates selective outcomes from forgetting outcomes: erasure uses spans
of branches, not coherent XOR, so branches are not canceled by forgetting. A probabilistic
system would generally aggregate alternatives using probabilities rather than this modal span
construction. Neither aggregation can be imported into the other system merely because both
describe “uncertainty.”
9.4 Possible reciprocal benefits
PALO’s discussion of modular theories and neural components [1, Section 5, pp. 47–48] gives
the process perspective a possible architectural application: specify interfaces for composition,
hidden state, conditioning, and discarded information. The process companion supplies a model
of the discipline required to check such interfaces.
Conversely, PALO could suggest a separate learned probabilistic layer over explicitly identified
supportobjects. Thatwouldneednormalizedprobabilities, compatibilityrules, andanoperational
interpretation beyond the source manuscripts. It would not turn ring amplitudes into probabilities
or establish a physical quantum model.

10.2 What a certified output should contain
A useful output is more than four truth bits. It should include the ordered logical inputs, polarity
convention, threshold and tie policy, declared domain, reachability information, guard, certificate
or proof reference, and a model/version identifier. A partial output should distinguish an actual
mixed-sign cell from a region that the analysis could not decide.
A complete operator dictionary can reduce required probes only after the dictionary’s coverage
and the relevant zero-free promise are established. Sampling examples of four operators does not
prove that a fifth cannot occur. Likewise, a high-confidence learned prediction of the operator is
a proposal until its stated certificate obligation is discharged.
10.3 Three meanings of “extending PALO”
1. Add a validation interface.Keep the original learning semantics and attach exact
analyses for selected abstractions. This is the least disruptive first project.
2. Add an alternative evaluation mode.Evaluate small Boolean blocks under explicit
joint distributions or dependence bounds. This changes the semantic options and needs its
own tests.
3. Change learning or compilation.Use extracted rules, exact blocks, or reformulated
axioms to change training. This can be valuable, but improvement is empirical and cannot
be inferred from logical equivalence.
These levels should be evaluated separately. Combining all of them in one initial experiment
would obscure whether a result came from the representation, additional information, a changed
objective, or a new optimizer.

The work below is a recommendation, not a report of completed experiments or an instruction

integration.

12.1 Stage 1: a semantic comparison harness
Question. Which operations agree among Boolean CM evaluation, PALO’s primitive soft
evaluation, and joint-distribution evaluation?
Design. Exhaust all sixteen binary truth tables and a rational grid of marginals; test Boolean
corners, signed-frame changes, repeated variables, complements, and shared subformulas. Com-
pare recursive soft evaluation with whole-function multilinear evaluation. Maintain separate
expected-agreement and expected-disagreement cases.
Decision gate.All intended equalities and counterexamples must be explained by a declared
contract. If a purported optimization alters soft values or gradients, relabel it as a change of
semantics before proceeding. The bounded calculations accompanying this report cover the basic
identities, not a production PALO integration.
12.2 Stage 2: a certified finite abstraction demonstrator
Question. Can a small learned score family yield useful exact operators on a meaningful part of
its domain?
Design. Start with a low-dimensional polynomial family or another model with a feasible sound
sign-analysis method. Reproduce the guard note’s four-operator example as calibration, then fit
a separately specified small target problem. Use either a finite domain with exact evaluations
or regional certificates with explicit coverage. Keep a direct truth-table implementation as the
baseline.
Metrics. Report certified coverage, actual boundary/tie cases, unresolved regions, certificate
generation cost, operator identification cost, and downstream task accuracy. False certificates
must be ruled out by the soundness mechanism, rather than estimated only by sampling. Record
the reachability and threshold policy.
Decision gate.Continue only if the certified output is useful enough to justify its cost. A result
that is slow but reliably distinguishes exact from unsupported interpretations can still have value;
it should not be sold as an acceleration.
12.3 Stage 3: a small dependence-aware block study
Question. Does explicit local joint information improve a PALO-style task enough to justify
collecting and maintaining it?
Design. Compare product evaluation, a joint-table oracle on synthetic data, estimated joint
tables, and feasible-set bounds. Include independent, correlated, repeated, and complementary
inputs. Use small blocks and a declared global data-generating distribution so that correctness is
measurable.
Metrics. Measure error against the known event probabilities, sensitivity to estimated corre-
lations, inference/training cost, and consistency failures across overlapping blocks. An oracle
using true joint information is an upper-reference condition, not a fair deployable competitor to
a method given only marginals.
Decision gate. Require benefits on held-out instances under matched information access. Do
not claim a representational gain when the improved system simply receives more information.

12.4 Stage 4: regularity and domain-abstraction experiments
Question. When does a desired exact sign rule conflict with the architecture’s regularity, and
how much does restricting the domain help?
Design. Contrast finite-point fit, margin-separated domain fit, and whole-orthant realization.

be integrated into a larger PALO-style workflow. Compare generic Boolean optimizers and table

them. They are intellectually related but would add substantial semantic machinery to an initial
prototype. The lowest-cost useful path is a small guarded extraction study, not an attempt to
unify all six manuscripts at once.

14 Recommended interpretation of the portfolio
The most coherent joint research story is a layered one: domain knowledge guides learning;
learned scores receive an explicit interpretation; suitable regions or finite abstractions admit
certified Boolean behavior; exact Boolean objects can then be represented and processed with
the CM/LM calculus. The compiler supplies a bounded implementation route, and finite-jet
analysis can reject certain overly strong specifications before costly experimentation.
PALO provides the learning setting, soft semantics, model exploration, and validation questions.
The portfolio provides exact representations and unusually explicit boundaries for extraction,
transformation, and composition. Their productive intersection is therefore a set of well-specified
interfaces. Its scientific value would be demonstrated by a small application that shows when
those interfaces improve reliability, interpretability, accuracy, or total cost.
The recommended next research step is Stage 1 followed by a tightly scoped Stage 2 demonstrator.
No manuscript revision, implementation campaign, external publication, or training expenditure is
required to use the present report. Historical priority, broad neural certification, and performance
advantage remain separate questions.

C Terminology for future collaboration
Exact Boolean representation. A representation of a Boolean function with a declared
variable frame. This says nothing about uncertain observations by itself.
Product-distribution evaluation.Expectation of a truth table under independent Bernoulli
inputs. It is exact for that distribution and approximate for another distribution unless the result
happens to agree.
Recursive soft evaluation.Numerical interpretation of a particular formula’s construction
using chosen soft connectives. Classical equivalence can be lost.
Certified extraction.A justified discrete interpretation on a declared domain under stated
threshold, reachability, and zero-handling rules.
Whole-orthant realization.A uniform strict sign requirement throughout each open orthant,
including arbitrarily close to a common corner where the score is defined with the specified
regularity.
Support semantics. A description of possible and impossible outcomes. It does not supply
their probabilities.
