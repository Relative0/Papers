Several features conspire to make these gradients well-suited for optimization. First, the domain
[−∞,+∞] is unbounded: unlike connectives valued in[0,1] or [0,+∞) , no amount of satisfaction or
violation drives operands into a saturated region where derivatives flatten out. Second, the gradients
align with logical intuition, i.e. conjunction gets gradients which focus the optimisation effort on the
most violated term. Third, the partial derivatives never vanish at finite operands—the contribution
of every subformula remains visible to the optimizer regardless of how thoroughly it is dominated
by its peers. This is theshadow-liftingproperty of Varnai and Dimarogonas [2020], and it is what
justifies preferring ∪p/∩p to their non-smooth counterparts min/max :
Proposition 3.2(Shadow-lifting).For p <∞ , the partial derivatives of ∩p and ∪p are strictly
positive for all finite values—no operand is ever ‘shadowed’ by the other.

Together, these properties give rise to what we calladversarially informative gradients. In adversarial
min-max optimisation, gradients must decrease as constraints become satisfied, yet not vanish entirely
so as to still expose nearby violations. As a consequence, gradient signal persists even for satisfied
constraints, enabling gradient-based methods such as PGD to find violations while still guiding
optimisation towards satisfaction.

4 Evaluation of QLL
In this section we evaluate QLL against other differentiable logics based on their ability to jointly
achieve high prediction performance and satisfy a given logical constraint on a classification task. As
a baseline, we consider standard supervised learning without any differentiable logics or adversarial
training, where models are trained solely to optimise prediction accuracy. Details on the experi-
mental setup, including datasets, model architectures, and evaluation methodology, are provided
in Appendix A.5.
The experiments were run using a reusable, general purpose framework for property-driven training
we implemented in PyTorch [Paszke et al., 2019]. The framework, including all code, data, and
scripts required to reproduce the results presented in this paper, is available at https://github.
com/tflinkow/property-driven-ml.
4.1 Experimental Set Up: Exhaustive Verification vs Testing and Adversarial Attacks
Given a logical constraint ϕ, there are three ways in which adherence of a model to the constraint can
be measured. We list them in the order of increasing rigor. The standard mathematical definitions
ofConstraint Security(CSec) andVerified Constraint Satisfaction(CSat), following [Casadio et al.,
2022], are given in Definitions A.1 to A.3 in Appendix A.6.
• Constraint Security( CSec) evaluates whether a PGD attack [Madry et al., 2018] can discover
counterexamples to ϕ in Bε( ˆx). This is thede factostandard ML method of finding examples
violating ϕ, see [Fischer et al., 2019]. Effectiveness of this search varies based on the loss, and it
comes with no formal guarantee of finding a counterexample if one exists7.
• Verified Constraint Satisfaction(CSat) evaluates whether ϕ holds for all possible samples in Bε( ˆx).
This measurement, also calledformal verification, is the most rigorous, and gives sound and
complete guarantees of finding a counterexample if one exists. Formal verification is carried out
with Marabou [Katz et al., 2019, Wu et al., 2024] and Vehicle [Daggitt et al., 2025].
All metrics are evaluated on the test sets. Standard prediction accuracy of models is denotedPrediction
Accuracy( PAcc). The CSat metric, which exhaustively analyses the entire Bε( ˆx) for a data point ˆx,
is obtained from a fixed randomly chosen subset of the test set. For all experiments, we report the
mean and standard deviation (in %) across 5 random seeds for all metrics.
Given a property ϕ, CSec, seen as robustness to attack on ϕ, is thus always an optimistic approxima-
tion of CSat forϕ. Our experiments in Tables 5 and 13 show that typically, this over-approximation
is vastly unrepresentative. However, the gap between CSec and CSat is consistently lower when
we train with QLL.
