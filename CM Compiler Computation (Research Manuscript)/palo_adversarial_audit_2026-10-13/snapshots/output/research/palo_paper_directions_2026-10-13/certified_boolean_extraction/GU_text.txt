Guarded Boolean Operators:
Exact Dispatch and a Polynomial Guard-Degree
Tradeoff
Brian Theory
Abstract
A finite family of continuous scores determines a Boolean operator wherever all scores are
nonzero. We study exact identification of that operator under an explicit guard language, using
the real polynomialF (x,y ) = x +y +xy on signed positive-magnitude representatives as a
complete case study. Its parameter domain has exactly four operator chambers, labelled by AND,
the two projections, and XNOR. In the original magnitude coordinates, letmd be the minimum
number of binary polynomial-sign tests of total degree at mostd needed for exact dispatch on
the zero-free domain. We provem1 =∞, m2 =m3 = 3, andmd = 2 for d≥ 4; moreover, both
polynomials in any two-test solution must have degree at least four. Reciprocal coordinates
linearize the chamber boundaries, making the coordinate dependence explicit. Three truth-table
coordinate probes remain necessary in the worst case, including for adaptive identification. We
place these calculations in a zero-aware certification contract, distinguish fixed test selection
from per-instance and adaptive costs, and prove directly that dispatch is unchanged by faithful
correspondence-matrix and fixed-frame logical-matrix representations. The general sign quotient
and minimum-test-set formulation are established machinery; the contribution is a self-contained
worked analysis of how coordinates, permitted observations, and zero handling change exact
dispatch.
1 Introduction
A continuous model can induce different Boolean operators as its parameters vary. For example,
evaluate the real scorex +y at (a,b ), (a,−b), (−a,b ), (−a,−b) with a,b> 0, and declare positive
output true and negative output false. Ifa>b , the resulting truth table is the first projection; if
b>a , it is the second. Ata =b, two scores vanish and the strict binary interpretation is undefined.
Thus even this elementary model requires both a parameter partition and a zero policy.
The broad passage from continuous parameters to discrete logical data is not new. DSGRN
constructs combinatorial parameter descriptions for switching regulatory networks and attaches
dynamical information to them [12, 13, 15]. Logical bifurcation diagrams relate parameter variation
in piecewise differential models to logical valuations and attractors [1]. Joint realizability and
parameter adjacency of monotone Boolean functions are treated in [11], and recent work embeds
monotone Boolean models into multilevel and DSGRN models [14]. These structured dynamical
theories have substantially more application-specific content than a generic score-sign partition.
Likewise, strict polynomial sign conditions and their connected components are standard real
algebraic geometry [4, 5, 16]; collecting sign semantics belongs to abstract interpretation [9]; and
selecting a minimum distinguishing test family is a classical optimization problem [17, 6]. Predicate
minimization in verification is related but can optimize preservation of a property rather than
exact recovery of every operator label [8]. We use these connections to specify the problem, not

Correspondence matrices (CMs) and logical matrices (LMs) provide optional output represen-

fact needed here. Readers interested only in dispatch may regard a CM simply as a reshaped truth
table. Classical trajectory and Walsh calibrations are collected in the appendix rather than used as
novelty claims.

3.2 The boundary obstruction
Lemma 3.1 (Boundary factors). The three quadraticsh1,h 2,h 3 in (1) are pairwise nonassociate
irreducibles in R[a,b ]. If finitely many nonzero polynomial zero sets coverCi, at least one of their
polynomials is divisible byhi. After constant tests are discarded, every exact finite polynomial-sign
library has such a factor for each ofi = 1, 2, 3.
Proof. Writehi =Ai(a)b +Bi(a). The coefficient pairs are
(A1,B 1) = (−a− 1,a ), (A2,B 2) = (1−a,−a), (A3,B 3) = (a− 1,−a).
In each pair the entries are coprime inR[a]. Thushi is primitive and is linear, hence irreducible,
over the fraction fieldR(a). Gauss’s lemma gives irreducibility inR[a,b ]. Comparing coefficients
shows that no two are scalar multiples.
Parametrize the corresponding positive arc byb =−Bi(a)/Ai(a) on an open interval avoiding
the pole. Substituting into any polynomialH(a,b ) gives a rational function ofa. Unless it vanishes
identically, it has only finitely many zeros on that interval. A finite collection of such finite sets
cannot cover the interval. Therefore someH vanishes identically after substitution. Division in
R(a)[b], followed by primitivity, shows thathi divides H in R[a,b ].
Finally, letz∈Ci. If every library polynomial were nonzero atz, all test values would be constant
on a sufficiently small neighborhood ofz. That neighborhood contains zero-free points from the
two different operator chambers adjoiningCi, contradicting exactness. Hence the library zero sets
coverCi, and the preceding argument supplies the required factor. This reasoning uses points on
the boundary only to compare nearby promised inputs; it does not assume that dispatch is defined
on ∆.

3.3 What the separation does not imply
The affine obstruction is not invariant under arbitrary changes of coordinates. Equations(4)–(5) give
an explicit smooth change under which the three original score signs are affine tests. Alternatively,
introducing the featurez =ab makes them linear in(a,b,z ) on the feature surfacez =ab. Either
transformation changes the permitted input features and therefore the observation contract.
Nor does two-test quartic dispatch establish a runtime improvement. Computingh1h3 and
h2h3 from the original scores uses those scores and two additional multiplications. The theorem
compares sign-information channels under a degree constraint; feature construction, arithmetic
sharing, numerical certification, and bit complexity must be charged separately in an implementation
study.

4 Zero-aware certification and partial abstraction
4.1 Nonempty guards and explicit coverage
A strict sign certificate on a guardG⊆ Λ proves eithergα> 0 throughout G or gα< 0 throughout
G. It may be an analytic proof, an exact real-algebraic certificate, or another sound verification
object. A sample point or a plot is not a certificate of a uniform sign.
Proposition 4.1 (Nonempty-guard soundness). LetG be nonempty. If one strict sign of every
gα is proved throughoutG, then G⊆ Λ◦ and G carries a unique exact Boolean operator and
correspondence tensor. A collection of such guards is exact on a declared domainD0⊆ Λ◦ when it
coversD0. Guards with the same operator may be merged by explicit union; overlapping nonempty
guards with certified labels cannot disagree on their intersection.
Proof. Strict signs exclude zeros and determine the truth value at every input. Choosing any point
of G proves that this truth table is realized, and the uniform certificates make it the same at every
point. Coverage and the assertions about unions and intersections follow pointwise.
Empty guards must be discarded or marked infeasible. All strict inequalities are vacuously
true there, so the operator is not uniquely determined by empty-guard semantics. This is why
nonemptiness is part of the statement rather than an implicit implementation preference.
Polynomial sign decomposition and quantifier elimination provide exact routes in principle [4, 16].
Interval methods and solver-based methods may leave cells uncertified. Such an outcome reports a
limit of the analysis; it is not evidence that both signs actually occur. The present note supplies no
general solver for arbitrary continuous scores.
4.2 Certified dictionaries and reduced observation
A complete, already proved operator dictionary can justify inference of unobserved signs.
Proposition 4.2 (Reduced certificate with an explicit promise). LetD0⊆ Λ◦ be a certified zero-free
domain and let the finite setO contain every Θλ for λ∈D0. LetG⊆D0 be nonempty. Suppose
strict signs on a selected setS⊆{ 0, 1}n are proved constant throughoutG, and exactly one member
Θ∈O has the corresponding restriction toS. Then every point ofG has operatorΘ, including the
truth values at unobserved inputs.
Proof. At any point ofG an induced total operator exists by the zero-free hypothesis, lies inO
by dictionary completeness, and agrees with the certified restriction. Uniqueness of the dictionary
match identifies it withΘ.

4.3 Collecting semantics for predicate cells
Synthetic representatives supply one score per Boolean input. Predicate abstraction of a concrete
state space need not do so. LetD be a state space,P :D→{ 0, 1}n a total predicate map, and
F :D→ R a total score. Define
Sα ={sgn0F (x) :x∈D, P(x) =α}⊆{− 1, 0, +1},
where sgn0 retains zero. The empty set means an unreachable cell. The singleton sets{−1} and
{+1} mean exact false and true outputs. A set containing0 exhibits a tie, and a set containing both
strict signs exhibits genuine two-sided variation. The last two properties can hold simultaneously.
Proposition 4.3 (Exactness on predicate cells). On a reachable cell,q◦F is everywhere defined
and constant exactly whenSα is one of{−1},{+1}. Under the declared full-cube convention, a
canonical total Boolean operator is induced exactly when every cell is reachable and satisfies this
singleton criterion. Otherwise one retains a reachability mask and exact/partial cell information,
unless a separate completion policy is declared.
Proof. The two singleton cases make the threshold defined and constant. Conversely, a defined
constant strict threshold permits neither a zero nor both strict signs. Values on unreachable inputs
are not fixed by the concrete model; adding them is a completion choice.
This is a collecting-semantics contract, not a new abstract domain [9]. In particular, checking
only existence of a positive score and existence of a negative score cannot distinguish an empty
cell from a nonempty all-zero cell: both tests are false in both cases. Reachability, zeros, genuine
variation, and a solver’sUNKNOWN response must remain separate.

6 Reproducibility, limitations, and conclusion
The companion archive contains the source of this note, a portable exact rational dispatcher, a
deterministic verification script, machine-readable results, reproduction commands, and an audit
crosswalk. Both coordinate and quartic dispatch modes return a separate boundary status; floating-
point inputs are rejected by the reference implementation to avoid silently replacing exact rational
semantics with approximate comparisons. The tests compare direct signed-input evaluation, the
expanded scores, the reciprocal normal form, and both dispatch modes. They also check boundary
points, the original recorded rational grid, dictionary separation, actual minterm evaluation, and
the classical Walsh witnesses.
These checks are bounded computations, not formal proof certificates for the topology or universal
polynomial lower bound. The latter rest on the proofs in Theorems 2.2 and 3.2. The package
documents exactly what was run; it does not claim solver benchmarks, a speedup over direct score
evaluation, or a completed general guard-synthesis implementation.
The example separates three issues that can otherwise be conflated. Geometry determines where
each operator is realized. An observation contract determines which tests may be used and how
they are counted. Certification determines when those observations justify a total Boolean output.
In the original coordinates, the exact degree–cardinality tradeoff(6) and the three-coordinate lower
bound make the contract dependence explicit. Reciprocal linearization prevents interpreting the
result as a coordinate-free nonlinear obstruction. Faithful CM/LM representations preserve dispatch
distinctions but do not strengthen the information available from the continuous model.
The general quotient, collecting semantics, finite test selection, and Walsh arrangement remain
classical or elementary components. A stronger algorithms or applications claim would require a
motivated model family, an implemented certificate-producing backend, and an evaluation that
charges feature construction and certification as well as sign queries. Those are not conclusions of
this note.

A.1 Crossings, event counts, and endpoint-relative intervals
Proposition A.1 (Single-entry crossing). Letγ : (−η,η )→ Λ be continuous. Suppose one score
has a fixed strict sign on(−δ, 0) and the opposite fixed strict sign on(0,δ ), while all other scores
are nonzero on(−δ,δ ). Then the two side operators differ at exactly that entry. If the composed
scores are differentiable, a simple zero of one score and nonzero values of all the others at0 suffice
after shrinkingδ.
Proof. Every other composed score keeps its sign by continuity. The distinguished entry alone
changes. A nonzero derivative at a zero gives opposite strict signs on sufficiently short punctured
sides.
A mere assertion that a continuous function “changes sign at zero” would not exclude oscillation.
The fixed-side hypotheses are therefore part of the statement. One-input adjacency is already a
feature of structured parameter graphs and threshold arrangements [11, 2].

the setS =γ−1(∆) is closed and the induced operator is constant on each connected component
of [0,T ]\S. If all points ofS are isolated relative to[0,T ], thenS is finite. The complementary
