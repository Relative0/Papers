# Research viability assessment — PL

Propositional Logical Topology: Observation, Distinguishability, and Inference

Assessment date: **2026-09-26**. Decision: **KEEP_EXPOSITION**. Suggested review order: **10 of 16**.

**Recommended form:** ProLT tutorial/expository article with a concrete observation application.

**External novelty confidence:** Low for core mathematical novelty; moderate potential usefulness as exposition.

## Decision and contribution

Keep this as an expository framework. The current core manuscript already calls itself a synthesis, and that is the defensible position. Finite signatures ordered by inclusion, their upward-open topology, specialization order, weighted Hamming-type distances and standard order complexes are established constructions. “Propositional Logical Topology” can be a useful organized language for them, but the name does not establish a new mathematical invariant.

Its strongest explanatory distinction is **positive observation**: retaining what is certified true differs from treating a complete truth assignment as a fully observed binary record. This can clarify information refinement, but a useful application must specify which predicates are observable, what a partial transcript means, and which states remain feasible. A topology on full semantic signatures alone is not already a model of an adaptive partial observation process.

## Mathematical boundaries worth preserving

| Construction | What is useful | What cannot be claimed |
|---|---|---|
| Signature inclusion and specialization | A clear finite order model of positive certification | New logical entailment beyond ordinary semantics |
| Weighted distances and directed loss | Application-dependent discrimination/cost | Determination by topology alone |
| Same state-predicate incidence as a formal context | A comparison with FCA | Every open set is automatically a formal extent |
| Two-sided observations | Connection to partition-style knowledge | Equivalence with positive-only specialization in general |
| Heyting operations on opens | A legitimate internal logic | “Take the interior” commutes with every classical formula |
| Observation-cover nerve | Useful presentation-dependent complex | A topology invariant independent of the chosen cover |

For example, repeating an observation or changing its weight can alter a metric without changing the topology. A union of observed truth sets need not be an FCA extent. Adding a tautological member can cone an observation nerve even when the underlying topology stays the same. Recursive Heyting evaluation must be distinguished from taking the interior of a precomputed classical truth set; double negation already exposes the difference. These are valuable teaching examples and should remain explicit.

[Vickers’ observation-oriented topology](https://sjvickers.github.io/papersfull.html) and the [observation-frame literature](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/1395) are clear antecedents. This review inspected primary descriptions/records rather than all corresponding book chapters, so an exact theorem-level crosswalk remains a publication preparation task. Nothing in the present evidence supports presenting the core as historically independent of those traditions.

## Relation to the other folders

BT has a much more focused new-looking theorem about a special order-thinning operation. GR addresses possible safe observation selection and carrier-changing refinement. Neither needs the entire ProLT core to count as mathematics, and their results should not also be claimed as separate core-paper discoveries. The historical observation-dimension equality from v0.5 is invalid; the corrected explicit predicate-cover formulation is recorded in GR, not rehabilitated by the core exposition.

The prior ledger’s corrections are therefore part of the value of the project, not an invitation to restore broader early claims. No cognitive, physical or quantum application is validated merely by identifying an order/topology analogy.

## Extensions worth considering

1. **Positive-only diagnosis or information acquisition.** Choose a small real application with partial observations, costs and ambiguous states. Specify the transcript update rule and compare the resulting decisions with FCA/rough-set/ordinary set-based alternatives. Success is a clearer or more useful analysis, or a precise new constrained-selection theorem. Stop if topology adds terminology without changing a question or answer.
2. **A tutorial with executable examples.** A reader should be able to build signatures, the T0 quotient, order complex, and an observation refinement, then see exactly which invariants change. This is a legitimate educational artifact; a generic visualization alone is not a research theorem.
3. **A reusable interface to safe-refinement certificates.** Explain how an application supplies the input expected by BT or GR. Keep the substantive selection/collapse result in its owner project rather than duplicating it here.

The next choice is whether the intended audience benefits from this exposition. If yes, a short attributed article or technical guide is appropriate. There is no present reason to seek a separate foundational-theory paper by accumulating more elementary propositions.
