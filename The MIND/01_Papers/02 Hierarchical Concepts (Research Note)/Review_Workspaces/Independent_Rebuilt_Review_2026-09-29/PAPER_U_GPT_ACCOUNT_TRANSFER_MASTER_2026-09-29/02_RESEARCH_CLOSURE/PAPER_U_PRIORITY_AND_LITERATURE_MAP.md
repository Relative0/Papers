# Paper U: priority and literature map

**Audit date:** 29 September 2026. **Status:** bounded primary-source search, not an exhaustive priority certificate.

## 1. Decisive findings

The mathematical architecture is not new. Three independent precedents are especially important:

1. **Khrennikov (2007), section 4:** fixed-prefix equivalence classes already represent associations. This is closer than simply "concepts are balls."
2. **Khrennikov and Yurova (2017), sections 4 and 9:** an observational equivalence quotient is combined with a p-adic/ultrametric latent state space. Changing the application from proteins to cognition does not restore architectural novelty.
3. **Kleiner (2019 preprint / 2020 article), section 3.4, equations (3.3)-(3.8):** automorphism-orbit equivalence describes accessible references to experience. The symmetry/observability connection is therefore not unique to Paper U either.

These sources do not establish that every formula in the revision has appeared verbatim. They do defeat an originality argument based on the conjunction of hierarchy, observation, equivalence, and symmetry alone. The finite ball quotient is standard mathematics. The adaptive normality criterion is a useful explicit deduction from standard full-tree automorphism theory; this search did not establish first priority for its exact statement.

## 2. Evidence grades and method

**A:** relevant primary full-text passages inspected. **A-OCR:** existing third-party OCR of a primary work, with printed page headers checked; scan images not independently verified. **B:** publisher/author abstract, metadata, contents, or limited preview only. **C:** discovery lead not used to establish a theorem. These grades concern access, not mathematical truth or publication quality.

Searches covered the prompt's eight families, followed named sources and citation trails, and screened recent 2023-2026 representation and observation papers. Primary texts and author repositories were preferred. No paid MathSciNet, zbMATH, PsycINFO, or comprehensive citation-index export was available. Negative keyword results do not establish absence of a construction. Regenerated arXiv HTML dates and repository modification dates were not treated as publication dates.

The general universal-property language does not manufacture novelty. With a fixed partition, a set-theoretic quotient already represents all cell-constant maps. A pseudometric quotient represents nonexpansive maps to metric spaces. A Kolmogorov quotient represents continuous maps to T0 spaces. The revision spells out these different categories; it does not conflate them into an unspecified "universal object."

## 6. Residual priority questions, precisely bounded

The search did not locate a named theorem stating exactly the adaptive normality criterion in Theorem 1.1. Its short proof and the standard tree-group ingredients make independent rediscovery quite plausible; lack of a located citation is not positive evidence of novelty. A full-tree group specialist should decide whether it is a familiar unstated corollary worth including or should simply be presented as an exercise-level lemma.

Access gaps remain for the 1997 subconscious chapter and the final 2010, 2014 and 2017 cognitive articles. The first-topological-repair claim is therefore explicitly unavailable. It is unnecessary for the chosen synthesis/correction form. The 2026 screen is current but bounded, not an all-databases or all-languages search.

## 7. Publication positioning

**Mathematical research novelty: not established.** **Useful synthesis/correction: supported.** A short expository or conceptual-methodology note is the coherent form. A pure group-theory or topology research journal would reasonably ask for substantially more than these standard mechanisms. A mathematical modeling/foundations or mathematics-and-psychoanalytic-theory venue family is more plausible, provided the manuscript is offered as a correction/synthesis and not empirical evidence. This is a venue-family judgment, not a checked acceptance prediction or a claim about any journal's current policies.
