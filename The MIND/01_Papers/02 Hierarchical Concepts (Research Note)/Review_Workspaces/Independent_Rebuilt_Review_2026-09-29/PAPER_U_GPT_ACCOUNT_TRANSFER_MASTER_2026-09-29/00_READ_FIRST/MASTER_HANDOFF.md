## 3. Strongest mathematical sharpening

For finite complete frontiers `F <= F'` of the full regular `m`-ary rooted tree, let `G_F` be the pointwise stabilizer of the old frontier, let `N` be the number of newly exposed internal vertices between the two frontiers, and let the source contract be exactly the full regular tree.

The revised theorem establishes the exact index

`[G_F : G_F'] = (m!)^N`.

It also identifies the precise boundary between mere finite index and a genuine quotient group:

`G_F'` is normal in `G_F` **iff**, inside each old frontier cylinder, the refinement proceeds to a single complete relative level. The relative level may differ from one old cylinder to another.

When normality holds, the quotient decomposes as a product of automorphism groups of the corresponding finite rooted trees.

A key counterexample is the binary adaptive frontier `{00, 01, 1}` below the root: the refined subgroup has finite index four but is **not normal**. Therefore “adaptive refinement gives a quotient group” is false without the full-level condition.

**Priority status:** useful and sharpened, but not certified novel. A fresh tree-group specialist must search for an exact named/citable predecessor.

## 4. Other mathematical results that survived

### Coordinate-free finite-ball collapse

For a finite partition of an ultrametric space into nonempty clopen balls, collapsing each cell to zero distance while preserving cross-cell distances gives a well-defined ultrapseudometric. This aligns the partition topology, topological indistinguishability/Kolmogorov quotient, zero-distance metric identification, and the factorization of observables through the finite quotient.

This is best treated as a clean synthesis of standard mechanisms, not automatically as a novel theorem.

### Resolution towers and completion

The corrected reconstruction statement distinguishes total boundedness from completeness. For a totally bounded ultrametric space, the inverse limit of finite resolution quotients reconstructs the **completion**. It reconstructs the original space itself when the space is complete/compact under the stated hypotheses.

Do not revert to the incorrect implication “totally bounded therefore recovered exactly.”

### Ultrametricity versus arbitrary partitions

An arbitrary partition provides observational equivalence, but ultrametric ball partitions contribute a compatible nested hierarchy and constant cross-distances between disjoint balls. This is what supports the exact distance-preserving collapse. The strengthening study contains a counterexample showing that the analogous collapse recipe can fail the triangle inequality for an arbitrary partition.

### Entropy versus symmetry index

Keep these notions separate. An equal stabilizer index need not imply equal information gain. The closure work includes a binary example where two frontiers have the same symmetry index but different entropies. Do not rename group index as entropy.
