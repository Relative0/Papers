Abstract
In this thesis we attempt to model ideas of Ignacio Matte-Blanco
and his ideas relating the unconscious to symmetry and consciousness
to asymmetry. We attempt to model the unconscious by means of
the p-Adic ultrametric. We then attempt to model consciousness by
the ’collapse’ of a quantum like superposition of propositional ”logic
waves”. Lastly we develop ideas of a ”mental space” which we give
a topology based on propositional logic, build simplical complexes of
logical expressions, and consider logical analogs to notions in algebraic
topology such as homotopy and homology.

Introduction
Recently there has been a large movement to ﬁnd a mathematical model of
the mind. Much of the progress made in developing a model has been on the
substrate level, such as that of neuronal interaction, modeled for example, by
neural networks in which the brain is a ’Black Box’ in which, through train-
ing, particulars of Brain functionality is reached. This methodology allows
for simulating various Brain functions. However, as in a computer simula-
tion, there is a problem with the neural networks ability to distinguish itself
from its environment and be conscious of its disassociation with it.
We approach the problem in a diﬀerent manner and investigate the pos-
sibility that consciousness is a natural eﬀect, built into the laws of nature;
while consciousness may evolve, and this author believes it does, we inves-
tigate the possibility that initial spark of consciousness may lie in nature’s
ability to create asymmetries from symmetries.
We look at abstract mathematics, logic, quantum mechanics, and work
done by Psychoanalyst Ignacio Matte-Blanco to build our model of the un-
conscious, conscious, as well as a topology of the mind.
This thesis is divided into 4 parts (sections):
Part 0: Math Basics. In this section we list ’basic’ ideas which we develop
on later in the thesis. The thesis has been structured such that notions which
are common to mathematics; that is, can be found in oﬀ the shelf graduate
or undergraduate text books or in online papers which give expositions on
various topics in mathematics.
Part 1: Mathematical Constructions. In this part we develop the tools
which will be used in the developing of the rest of the thesis work. The math-
ematical constructions found here were developed from basic ideas given in
part 0 and may or may not be found in other works as they were developed
speciﬁcally for the latter parts of the thesis; hence there will not be much in
terms of references except to that which the author has found useful in the
development of these mathematical constructions.

0.1 Logic
Motivation: In the study of logic one employs a set of axioms in order to
deﬁne a system. Letting the mind be treated as the system and the axioms
be those of (Aristotelian) logic we aim to ultimately express mental processes
in terms of a logic.
We ﬁrst must deﬁne some concepts.
Deﬁnition (Logical Expression)
A logical expression L is a declarative statement which either has a true
or false value. The set of logical expressions in CNF (Conjunctive Normal
Form) is written L. ♠
Deﬁnition (Atom)
An atom or an atomic formula is a single element of a logical expression
which contains no logical structure or connectives, or equivalently atoms are
formulas which contain no strict sub-formulas. ♠
Deﬁnition (Logical Negation)
Let L ∈ L be a logical expression. The logical negation of L denoted ¬L
is the operation ¬ which gives L the opposite truth valuation. ♠
Deﬁnition (Literal)
A literal is an atomic formula (atom) or its negation. Thus literals have
two forms, ﬁrst either a positive literal which is an atom, or a negative literal
which is just the negation of an atom. ♠

A propositional variable or logical operand is a variable in a logical expres-

Deﬁnition (Truth Values)
The set of truth values written T is the set of values T = {F, T }where
F = False and T = True. ♠
Deﬁnition (Logical Operator)
A logical operator is an operator (function) f : (T, T) → T. ♠

the logical literals and the last column to the logical expression in which the
truth values are assigned. The rows of the truth table are those of unique
sets of truth assignments to logical literals. ♠

0.2 Topology
Motivation: In mathematics we have the notion of aTopologyto model spaces

Deﬁnition (Basic Neighborhood)
Let X be a non-empty set, and suppose that for each x ∈ X there exists

properties:
1. If x ∈ U ∀U ∈ Ux;
2. If U ∈ Ux and V ∈ Ux then ∃W ∈ Ux such that W ⊂ U ∩ V ;
3. If y ∈ U and U ∈ Ux then ∃V ∈ Uy with V ⊂ U
Each U ∈ Ux is called a basic neighborhood of x. ♠

0.3 Ultrametric
Motivation: A metric is a relationship between points to give the notions of
distance. If we take two points in space, a space that we observe or that
we just make up (after all even the space we observe is modeled by one that
we have made) we would like to know things such as if the points are path
connected. Ultimately we want to understand how thoughts and ideas are
connected in our mental space as well as, for example, how close one idea is
to another.
Deﬁnition (Metric)
A metric on a set X is a function:
d : X × X → R such that ∀x, y, z ∈ X
(1) d(x, y) ≥ 0
(2) d(x, y) = 0 iﬀ x = y
(3) d(x, y) = d(y, x)
(4) d(x, y) ≤ d(x, z) + d(z, y) ♠

centered at point p ∈ M we deﬁne:
a open metric ball :
Br(p, r) = {x ∈ M |d(x, p) < r }

4 Conclusion
For Logical Constructions:
• Developed some logical structures such as the (Linear) Correspondence
Matrices and was able to embed them in to more familiar Tensors and Bra-
Ket notation so as to use in other areas of mathematics.
• Developed a way in which to view propositional logical expressions in terms
of quantum mechanical principles.
• Found interesting operations such as matrix rotation which allow logical
transformations. A possible algorithm for faster computations concerning
propositional logical expressions?
For Matte-Blanco’s theory of the Mind :
• Developed the notion of ”Concepts” using simplical complexes which al-
lows for the relation of information in a ”Mental Space”.
• Found a possible model for the ”Unconscious” using Ultrametrics (Non-
Archimedean Geometry) which allows for symmetries and indistinguishabil-

• Found a possible model for the ”Conscious” using principles of quantum
