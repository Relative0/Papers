CONSTRUCTIONS OF URYSOHN UNIVERSAL
ULTRAMETRIC SPACES
YOSHITO ISHIKI
Abstract. In this paper, we give new constructions of Urysohn
universal ultrametric spaces. We ﬁrst characterize a Urysohn un i-
versal ultrametric subspace of the space of all continuous func-
tions whose images contain the zero, from a zero-dimensional com-
pact Hausdorﬀ space without isolated points into the space of non-
negative real numbers equipped with the nearly discrete topology.
As a consequence, the whole function space is Urysohn universal,
which can be considered as a non-Archimedean analog of Banach–
Mazur theorem. As a more application, we prove that the space of
all continuous pseudo-ultrametrics on a zero-dimensional compac t
Hausdorﬀ space with an accumulation point is a Urysohn univer-
sal ultrametric space. This result can be considered as a variant of
Wan’s construction of Urysohn universal ultrametric space via the
Gromov–Hausdorﬀ ultrametric space.
1. Introduction
For a class C of metric spaces, a metric space ( X,d ) is said to be C-
injective if for all (A,a ) and (B,b ) in C and for all isometric embeddings
φ : (A,a ) → (B,b ) and ψ : (A,a ) → (X,d ), there exists an isometric
embedding θ: (B,b ) → (X,d ) such that θ ◦φ = ψ . This deﬁnition
roughly coincides with the notion of injective objects in category th eory.
We denote by F the class of all ﬁnite metric spaces. Urysohn [35]
constructed a complete separable F-injective metric space U, which is
nowadays called the Urysohn universal metric space . In this paper,
we investigate an ultrametric analogue of U. For more information
of U and related spaces, we refer the readers to, for instance, [28],
[12], [21], [25], [36], [30] and [31]. In the recent years, the theory of
Urysohn type metric spaces has applications to logic and model theo ry
(see for example, [3] and [4]). Indeed, the space U can be regarded
as a generalization of the random graph in model theory (see also [28 ,
Subsection 3.3]).

it satisﬁes the strong triangle inequality d(x,y ) ≤ d(x,z )∨d(z,y ), where

2. Preliminaries
In this section, we prepare some basic statements on ultrametrics ,
spaces of ultrametrics, and injective metric spaces.
2.1. Generalities. For a set E, the symbol Card( E) stands for the
cardinality of E. For a metric space ( X,d ),a ∈ X, and r ∈ (0, ∞ ), we
denote by B(a,r ;d) (resp. U(a,r ;d)) the closed ball (resp. open ball)
centered at a with radius r. We also deﬁne S(a,r ;d) = {x ∈ X |
d(x,a ) = r }. We often simply represent them as B(a,r ), U(a,r ),and
S(a,r ). In this paper, for a metric space ( X,d ), and a subset A of X,
we sometimes represent the restricted metric d|A2 as the same symbol
to the ambient metric d when no confusions can arise.
2.1.1. Dimension. A subset of a topological space is clopen if it is
closed and open in the ambient space. We deﬁne the zero-dimensiona lity
of topological spaces in three ways. Let X be a non-empty topological
space. We write ind(X) = 0 if X has an open base consisting of clopen
subsets of X. We also write Ind( X) = 0 if for all two disjoint closed
subsetsA andB ofX, there exists a clopen subset C such that A ⊆ C
andB ∩C = ∅. The notation dim( X) = 0 means that every ﬁnite open
covering ofX has a reﬁnement covering of X consisting of ﬁnitely many
mutually disjoint open subsets of X. The dimensions ind( X), Ind(X),
and dim(X) are called the small inductive dimension , the large induc-
tive dimension , and the covering dimension, respectively. Of cause, we
can deﬁne higher dimensional spaces, however, we omit it since we fo -
cus only on the 0-dimensionality in this paper. In general, these thre e
dimensions can diﬀerent from each other. However, as stated in th e
next proposition, they are all equal to 0 if X is compact Hausdorﬀ and
any one of the three is 0. The proof is deduced from [29, Corollary 2.2 ,
Proposition 2.3, pp156–157].
Proposition 2.1. LetX be a non-empty compact Hausdorﬀ space. If
any one of the three ind(X), Ind(X), and dim(X) is equal to 0, then
ind(X) = Ind(X) = dim(X) = 0 .

2.1.2. Topological weights. For a topological space X, we denote by
w(X) the topological weight of X. Namely, w(X) is equal to the mini-
mum of cardinals of all open bases of X (remark that some authors call
the cardinal max {w(X), ℵ 0} the topological weight). Hence a metriz-
able spaceX is separable if and only if w(X) ≤ ℵ 0. Using compactness,
we obtain the following lemma:
Lemma 2.2. LetX be a 0-dimensional compact Hausdorﬀ space such
that w(X) is inﬁnite. If C is the set of all clopen subsets of X, then
Card(C) = w(X).
For a metric space ( X,d ), we say a subset A of X is r-separated if
we have r ≤ d(x,y ) for all distinct x,y ∈ A. The following lemma can
be proven by the deﬁnition of the topological weights.
Lemma 2.3. Let (X,d ) be a metric space. If a subset E of X is
r-separated, then we have Card(E) ≤ w(X).
The next lemma can be proven by Lemma 2.3 with taking a maximal
(2− n)-separated set from a dense subset for each n ∈ Z≥ 0, or by the
fact that every metric space has a σ-discrete open base.
Lemma 2.4. Let (X,d ) be a metric space. If F is a dense subset of
X, then w(X) ≤ Card(F ).
Remark that Lemmas 2.3 and 2.4 are consequences of the fact that
the weight of a metric space is equal to the minimum of cardinals of
all dense subsets of the space.

2.2. Tenuous sets. A subset E of [0, ∞ ) is said to be semi-sporadic if
there exists a strictly decreasing sequence {ai}i∈ Z≥ 0 in (0, ∞ ) such that
limi→∞ ai = 0 and E = {0} ∪ { ai |i ∈ Z≥ 0 }. This concept is a half
version of sporadic sets deﬁned in [18]. A subset of [0 , ∞ ) is tenuous if
it is ﬁnite or semi-sporadic. Despite its simplicity, this concept has a
central role in the present paper.

topology satisfying that K ∩ [r, ∞ ) is ﬁnite for all r ∈ (0, ∞ ).

2.5. Injective ultrametric spaces. We show some properties of in-
jective ultrametric spaces. For a class C of ﬁnite metric spaces, we say
that a metric space ( X,d ) satisﬁes the one-point extension property for
C if for every ﬁnite metric space ( A ⊔ {ω },e ) in C (with the speciﬁc
point ω ), and for every isometric embedding φ : A → X, there exists
a point q ∈ X such that d(φ(a),q ) = e(a,ω ) for all a ∈ A. Using
induction, we obtain:
Lemma 2.47. Let C be a class of ﬁnite metric spaces. A metric space
(X,d ) is C-injective if and only if it satisﬁes the one-point extensio n
property for C.
The next lemma gives a more practical condition for the injectivity.
Lemma 2.48. Let C be a class of ﬁnite ultrametric spaces. An ultra-
metric space (X,d ) is C-injective if and only if it satisﬁes the following
property:
(Ex) Let (A ⊔ {ω },e ) be an arbitrary ﬁnite metric space in C and
φ : A → X be an isometric map. Let p ∈ A be a point satisfying
that e(p,ω ) = min a∈ Ae(a,ω ). Then there exists a point q ∈ X
such that if a ∈ A satisﬁes e(a,ω ) = e(p,ω ), then e(a,ω ) =
d(φ(a),q ).
Proof. It suﬃces to show that if ( X,d ) satisﬁes the property (Ex),
then it is C-injective. Let ( A ⊔ {ω },e ) be an ultrametric space in C,
and φ : A → X be an isometric embedding. Using the property (Ex),
we obtain q ∈ X such that if a ∈ A satisﬁes e(a,ω ) = e(p,ω ), then
e(a,ω ) = d(φ(a),q ). Notice that e(p,ω ) = d(φ(p),q ). To show that
(X,d ) satisﬁes the one-point extension property for C, take a ∈ A.
If e(p,ω ) = e(a,ω ), then the property of q ∈ X implies e(a,ω ) =
d(φ(a),q ). If e(p,ω ) < e(a,ω ), then Lemma 2.6 implies e(p,a ) =
e(a,ω ). Due to d(φ(p),q ) = e(p,ω ) and d(φ(p),φ (a)) = e(p,a ), we
have d(φ(p),q ) < d(φ(p),φ (a)). According to Lemma 2.6 again, we
obtain d(φ(a),q ) = d(φ(a),φ (p)). From e(p,a ) = e(a,ω ) and e(a,ω ) =

d(φ(a),q ) = e(a,ω ). Therefore ( X,d ) satisﬁes the one-point extension
property for C, and hence it is C-injective. □

5. Additional remarks
For an inﬁnite cardinal κ, let N(κ) denote the countable product of
the discrete space κ, which is sometimes called the 0 -dimensional Baire
space of weight κ. Notice that N(ℵ 0) is homeomorphic to the space of
irrational numbers. The following is a topological characterization o f
N(κ) (see [34, Theorem 1]).
Theorem 5.1. Let κ be an inﬁnite cardinal and X be a completely
ultrametrizable space. Assume that
(1) the space X has a dense subset of cardinal κ;
(2) every non-empty open subset of X contains a closed discrete
space of cardinal κ.
Then the space X is homeomorphic to the 0-dimensional Baire space
N(κ) of weight κ.
Remark 5.1. A topological space X satisﬁes the conditions (1) and (2)
in Theorem 5.1 if and only if every non-empty open subset O of X
satisﬁes w(O) = κ.
In [23, 24], Koshino determined the topological types of the spaces
Met(X) of metrics on a metrizable space X generating the same topol-
ogy of X equipped with the uniform topology or the compact-open
topology. We now show a non-Archimedean analogue of Koshino’s
work.
Theorem 5.2. Let κ be an inﬁnite cardinal, X be a 0-dimensional
compact Hausdorﬀ space with w(X) = κ possessing no isolated points,
and Y be a 0-dimensional compact Hausdorﬀ space with w(X) = κ
possessing an accumulation point. Let R be a characteristic range set.
Put τ = max {κ, Card(R)}. If either of Card(R) ≤ κ or Card(R ∩
[0,r ]) = Card( R) for all r ∈ R \ {0}, then C0(X,R ) and Cpu(X,R )
are homeomorphic to N(τ).
Proof. Based on Theorem 5.1, the case of C 0(X,R ) is deduced from
Propositions 2.25 and 2.26. Similarly, the case of Cpu( X,R ) follows
from Propositions 2.45 and 2.46. □
Corollary 5.3. For every countable characteristic range set R, the R-
Urysohn universal ultrametric spaces is homeomorphic to th e space of
irrational numbers.
