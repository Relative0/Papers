# Independent hostile reconstruction

## 1. Balls, collapse, and exact universal properties

Let B be an open ball of radius r centered at b. If x,x' belong to B and y does not, then d(x,x')<r<=d(b,y). Also d(x,y)=d(b,y): otherwise the strong triangle inequality in one direction or the other contradicts d(b,x)<d(b,y). Thus d(x,x')<d(x,y). For a closed ball, replace the intermediate inequalities by d(x,x')<=r<d(b,y); the same conclusion follows. A radius-zero closed ball is a singleton. Repeating the argument in a disjoint ball C proves that d is constant on B x C. No compactness, common radius, or regular branching is involved. Lemma 2.1 is correct.

For three distinct cells, representative independence permits selecting one point in each; their three distances satisfy the strong triangle inequality. If two cells coincide, the relevant quotient distances are either equal or zero, so the inequality still holds. Hence delta_P is an ultrametric. Pulling it back along q proves the collapse formula is an ultrapseudometric, including all repeated-cell cases. For a finite quotient with at least two cells, the minimum positive intercell distance is positive, so each cell is a small open ball for d_P. For a one-cell quotient, d_P=0 and its topology is indiscrete, also exactly tau_P.

For any pseudometric e<=d vanishing within cells, e(x,y)<=d_P(x,y) separately inside and across cells. Since d_P is itself admissible, it is the greatest such e. This proof does not assume e ultrametric. A stronger useful observation is that the triangle inequality for e already forces e to be constant on each B x C, because e(x,x')=e(y,y')=0. There is no hidden choice of representative in the maximality assertion.

The underlying set of all three quotients is P, but their source structures must be distinguished:

* Set quotient: q:X->P. Its universal factors are exactly cell-constant maps to an arbitrary set Y.
* Kolmogorov quotient: q:(X,tau_P)->P with P finite discrete. An f continuous into T0 Y identifies points with the same open neighborhoods: if their images differed, a distinguishing open subset of Y would contradict continuity. Its unique factor is continuous because P is discrete. Conversely any map out of discrete P is continuous, hence gives a continuous cell-constant map on (X,tau_P).
* Metric identification: q:(X,d_P)->(P,delta_P), quotienting zero distance. This is an isometry after identification, and its quotient topology is discrete.
* Original metric source: q:(X,d)->(P,delta_P) is nonexpansive. A cell-constant nonexpansive f:(X,d)->Y metric factors nonexpansively because each intercell distance is exactly retained. No claim that every continuous f:(X,d)->Y factors is valid or made.

Counterexample to that last unauthorized strengthening: use binary infinite sequences and the one-cell partition. The identity into the Hausdorff metric space X is continuous and not constant; it cannot factor through the one-point quotient. Counterexample to replacing T0 by arbitrary targets: the identity from a two-point indiscrete cell to the two-point indiscrete space is continuous and not constant. The stated theorem avoids both errors. Indicators into {0,1} distinguish any two cells, establishing the final observable equivalence.

The paper's arbitrary-partition counterexample is correct: collapsing 00 with 10 while retaining their distances to 01 yields 1<=0+1/2, a contradiction. Equilateral spaces show ball partitions are sufficient, not necessary. The theorem is correct; the phrase defining ultrapseudometric at TeX line 77 could explicitly include nonnegativity and zero diagonal, but the conventional term and the formula make this a minor definitional omission, not a false theorem.

## 2. Thresholds, topology, and completion

For r>0, the relation d<=r is an equivalence relation. Its classes are closed r-balls. Around each point in such a class the open r-ball stays inside the class, so it is also open. A finite cover by open balls of radius r (or smaller) proves finiteness of the class set in a totally bounded space. The one-point space is covered by the same reasoning, with any strictly decreasing positive radii.

For the collapse at level k, a pair is unchanged if d>r_k and becomes zero otherwise. Therefore 0<=d-d_k<=r_k and d_k<=d_(k+1). Refinement gives tau_k subset tau_(k+1). Every d-open neighborhood of x contains [x]_k for k large enough. Conversely all such cells are d-open. Thus the generated topology, not generally the bare union, is the original topology. With q_0 constant, the first differing coordinate kappa exists for x!=y and means precisely d>r_kappa and d<=r_(kappa-1).

Construct the completion identification without assuming surjectivity from X. A thread a consists of nested nonempty cells A_k. Choose x_k in A_k. For j>=k, x_j and x_k lie in A_k, so d(x_j,x_k)<=r_k; this sequence defines a completion point, independently of the choices. Two threads that first differ at k have their chosen representatives, from level k onward, at one fixed positive distance delta_k. Their completion points are distinct and at that distance. Every completion point has a Cauchy sequence from X; at each fixed k, the finite positive separation of its cells forces the sequence eventually to lie in just one cell. These eventual cells are compatible. The two constructions are inverse and give an isometry for D(a,b)=lim delta_k(a_k,b_k).

This also directly proves topology compatibility: agreeing at coordinate k forces D<=r_k; different cells at fixed k have a positive minimum separation, so coordinate cylinders are D-open. Threads from X meet every cylinder because its cell is nonempty. The completion is compact by total boundedness plus completeness; equivalently the inverse limit is a closed subset of the product of finite discrete spaces. The completion metric uses the retained delta_k data, not the bare inverse system of sets. Proposition 3.1 states the metric in its proof; no false canonicity claim remains once that specification is included.

Eventually-zero binary sequences provide a concrete obstruction to replacing completion by X: prefixes 1,11,111,... form a compatible thread with no member in X. This is a missing point, not noninjectivity. The manuscript supplies that counterexample correctly.

For the regular prefix metric, d<=lambda^k iff at least k initial digits agree, and d<lambda^k iff at least k+1 agree. At k=0 the closed unit ball is the whole boundary, whereas the open unit ball fixes the first digit. A length-k cylinder for k>=1 is the open ball of radius lambda^(k-1). Thus kappa=ell+1. The exact distance equality is false for an arbitrary scale calibration, but the paper only asserts the bounds there. For prime p, a first differing digit at index ell makes the p-adic difference p^ell times a unit, proving the stated isometry; coherent residues recover the digits. The zero-th residue ring is the trivial ring Z/Z, as required.

## 3. Full regular-tree frontier stabilizers

Use a local permutation (a portrait) at every vertex to describe a rooted automorphism. Fixing every frontier leaf forces identity local permutations at every proper prefix: every child subtree has a frontier descendant and cannot be exchanged with a different child subtree. Below each frontier leaf portraits are unrestricted. This proves G_F=product_s Aut(T_m), including F={epsilon} and empty I(F). Within one such unrestricted subtree a sequence of local permutations maps any ray to any other. Its cylinder is therefore one boundary orbit.

For the index, truncate at any depth L at least the maximum depth of F'. The common level-L pointwise stabilizer is contained in G_F'. Finite rooted automorphism groups have independent choices of m! local permutations at each vertex of depth <L. For G_F, all choices above F are fixed; for G_F', exactly N further vertices have their choices fixed. Thus the ratio of finite group orders, and hence the infinite-group index, is (m!)^N. This finite-quotient argument does not presuppose normality of G_F' in G_F and differs from the paper's stepwise argument.

A useful independent normalizer formulation is N_Aut(T_m)(G_E)={g:g(E)=E} for every complete finite frontier E. Indeed conjugation sends G_E to G_(gE); the boundary orbit partitions of these groups are the respective cylinder families. Distinct vertices in a full m>=2 tree have distinct cylinders: incomparable cylinders are disjoint, and a strict descendant omits a nonempty sibling cylinder. Therefore equality of groups forces equality of frontiers. The converse follows directly from conjugating point stabilizers.

Consequently G_F' is normal in G_F iff G_F permutes F'. In each old cell s, its full section is transitive on every relative level. If one relative-depth-h leaf occurs in F', invariance forces every leaf at that relative depth. Completeness and prefix freeness then exclude both shorter and longer frontier leaves in that old cell. Conversely if the descendants form s Sigma^h_s, every old-cell automorphism preserves them; equivalently the new stabilizer is the kernel of the onto restriction to product_s Aut(T_(m,h_s)). This proves both directions and the stated quotient. No inference from finite experiments is needed.

Edge cases and adversarial examples:

* h_s=0: the frontier keeps s; its factor contributes index 1 and quotient Aut(T_(m,0))=1. If F'=F, N=0 and all conclusions hold.
* Unequal levels across OLD cells are allowed. For binary F={0,1}, take F'={00,01,100,101,110,111}. Relative heights are 1 and 2; N=1+3=4, index=16, quotient S_2 x Aut(T_(2,2)). A global equal-depth requirement would be false.
* m=3: F={epsilon}, F'={00,01,02,1,2} has N=2 and index 36. A root permutation exchanging 0 and 1 fails to preserve the new frontier, proving nonnormality. For a uniform height-2 ternary refinement, N=1+3=4, index=6^4=1296, and normality holds.
* Simultaneous splits of a subset S of old leaves give independent relative heights 1 or 0; quotient S_m^|S|, including the empty subset and m>2.
* Pointwise matters. For F=Sigma_m (the first level), the SETWISE frontier stabilizer is the whole tree automorphism group, with a root S_m action missing from the paper's pointwise product. For binary F={0,1} and F'={00,01,1}, the pointwise index is 2 and the inclusion is normal; with setwise stabilizers the old group is the full group, the new setwise stabilizer fixes the root children and is the level-1 stabilizer, again normal but of a different nature. A sharper index distinction is F={epsilon}, F'=Sigma_m: both setwise stabilizers are the full group, giving index 1 instead of m!. Thus the theorem cannot be transferred to setwise stabilizers.
* Proper subgroups: let H be the binary level-1 stabilizer and take F={epsilon}, F'={00,01,1}. H intersect G_F'=G_F' is normal in H with quotient S_2, even though the refinement has mixed relative depths from the root. Thus necessity of the full-group criterion fails after restriction to a proper subgroup. For H={1}, all indices are 1 and all inclusions normal; its boundary orbits are singletons. Arbitrary subgroup sections can also be correlated rather than independent.
* Nonregular example: a root with two nonisomorphic infinite descendant trees, one full binary and one full ternary, cannot exchange its children. Splitting only the binary child gives a mixed-depth frontier whose stabilizer is nevertheless normal, with index 2 rather than a universal factorial count over all vertices. Full-group level transitivity, not merely having a rooted tree, is crucial. The paper's three-point distance example likewise has isometry group S_2 fixing c: c alone has no other point at distance 1/2, so it cannot lie in the orbit of a or b.

The central adaptive theorem is correct. The normalizer formulation is useful explanation, not a necessary repair. The manuscript's orbit-invariance proof is complete once one notes the injectivity of vertex-to-cylinder; this is an elementary implicit step, not an invalid theorem.

## 4. Frontier appendix

Finite complete frontiers correspond bijectively to finite downward-closed sets of vertices, allowing the empty set. A finite full subtree with I internal vertices and L leaves has mI edges and I+L-1 edges, hence L=1+(m-1)I. Refinement corresponds to inclusion of internal sets, so intersection gives meet and union gives join. These are finite and downward closed, and their set identities prove distributivity. Empty internal set gives the bottom frontier; adding any leaf as internal gives a strict refinement, so there is no finite top.

Kraft equality follows by finite disjoint additivity of uniform cylinder measure. Conversely the complement of finitely many cylinders is open (each cylinder is clopen). If nonempty it contains a cylinder of strictly positive uniform measure; equality of total measure to 1 excludes that. Finiteness is doing real work here: the infinite prefix code {0,10,110,...} in the binary tree has Kraft sum 1 yet misses the all-ones ray. This is not a counterexample to the finite statement.

For finite distinct A, each pair has a finite common prefix, so the union over pairs of their common-prefix sets is finite and is exactly I_A. It is downward closed. Any separating frontier E must have I_A subset I(E): if v holds at least two samples, stopping at or above v would fail separation. The frontier of I_A is itself separating because a leaf with two samples would have to belong to I_A. Thus it is the unique least element in the refinement order, a stronger conclusion than minimal leaf count. For |A|=0 or 1, I_A is empty and the one-leaf root frontier is correct. All appendix claims pass.

## 5. Entropy and index

For a split of mass w>0 into masses w p_i, subtracting the old entropy term gives -sum_i w p_i(log_2 w+log_2 p_i)+w log_2 w=w H(p). If w=0, all child masses vanish, and the contribution is zero without assigning conditional probabilities. Each newly internal vertex is split exactly once, so telescoping gives equation (8) for arbitrary probability measures on the cylinder sigma-algebra. No independence assumption is needed for this identity.

For uniform digits, w=m^(-|v|) and H(p)=log_2 m. The balanced binary frontier has four masses 1/4 and entropy 2. The comb has masses 1/8,1/8,1/4,1/2 and entropy 3/8+3/8+1/2+1/2=7/4. Both arise from the root with three splits and index 2^3=8. Here initial entropy is zero; for a general starting frontier the equation concerns a difference. At positive depth even a single binary split contributes less than one bit for the uniform measure while log_2 index remains one. Correctness does not depend on the comparison citation to hierarchical information theory.
