# Literature and Priority Map
## Search horizon: through 28 September 2026

This is a structure-first priority search. It does not assume that later authors use the thesis's vocabulary. The relevant mathematical neighborhoods are:

- Matte-Blanco / bi-logic and mathematical formalization;
- ultrametric mental spaces and category hierarchies;
- simplicial models of knowledge/concepts;
- object-attribute relations, Dowker complexes and Formal Concept Analysis;
- Boolean differential calculus;
- nerves, Čech complexes and reconstruction;
- sheaf/cosheaf gluing and contextuality;
- persistent and zigzag homology;
- topological models in neuroscience/cognition;
- conceptual spaces.

The priority judgments below are deliberately conservative. "No direct antecedent found" means none was located in this search; it is not a proof of novelty.

---

# 1. Matte-Blanco and ultrametric mental spaces

## 1.1 Andrei Yu. Khrennikov — mental space on \(m\)-adic trees

**Reference:** A. Yu. Khrennikov, "Toward an adequate mathematical model of mental space: conscious/unconscious dynamics on m-adic trees," *BioSystems* 90(3), 656–675 (2007). DOI: **10.1016/j.biosystems.2007.02.004**.

**Relevance:** Very high to the thesis's ultrametric mental-space program. The paper represents information states by points in a hierarchical \(m\)-adic mental space, associations by balls, and ideas by collections of balls, and studies conscious/unconscious information dynamics.

**Priority implication:** The general idea "mental categories/ideas as ultrametric objects with hierarchical conscious/unconscious dynamics" predates the 2015 thesis.

## 1.2 Andrei Yu. Khrennikov — categories encoded by ultrametric balls

**Reference:** A. Yu. Khrennikov, "Modelling of psychological behavior on the basis of ultrametric mental space: Encoding of categories by balls," *p-Adic Numbers, Ultrametric Analysis and Applications* 2(1), 1–20 (2010). DOI: **10.1134/S2070046610010012**.

**Relevance:** Extremely high to the thesis's order-\(k\) category construction. It explicitly encodes basic mental entities/categories by ultrametric balls.

**Priority implication:** The thesis's use of fixed initial \(p\)-adic digits as hierarchical categories should be treated as adaptation/extension rather than a new foundational construction.

## 1.3 Fionn Murtagh — Matte-Blanco, metric and ultrametric hierarchy

**Reference:** F. Murtagh, "Mathematical Representations of Matte Blanco's Bi-Logic, based on Metric Space and Ultrametric or Hierarchical Topology: Towards Practical Application," *Language and Psychoanalysis* 3(2), 40–63 (2014). DOI: **10.7565/landp.2014.008**.

**Relevance:** The closest pre-2015 source found that explicitly links Matte-Blanco's bi-logic to metric/ultrametric or hierarchical topology.

**Priority implication:** A paper now built from the thesis must cite this work prominently when discussing ultrametric/hierarchical mathematical representations of Matte-Blanco.

## 1.4 Recent Matte-Blanco literature

**Reference:** M. Goldreich, "Fearful symmetry in altered states: a bi-logic account of psychedelic action," *Frontiers in Psychology* 16 (2025), 1632923. DOI: **10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1632923**.

**Relevance:** Current evidence that Matte-Blanco's bi-logic remains an active conceptual framework. This is not a topological or homological formalization.

**Priority implication:** Useful for current context, but not an antecedent to the mathematical reconstruction proposed here.

### Priority conclusion for this cluster

**Prior art is strong for ultrametric mental spaces and mathematical treatments of Matte-Blanco.** The plausible new direction is not "use ultrametrics for the unconscious" but the exact algebraic/topological treatment of successive unfolding/refinement and reconstruction.

# 2. Simplicial models of concepts, knowledge and semantics

## 2.1 Valencia and Giavitto — algebraic topology for knowledge representation

**Reference:** E. Valencia and J.-L. Giavitto, "Algebraic Topology for Knowledge Representation in Analogy Solving," conference work from the late 1990s (ECAI-era; versions indexed 1998/1999).

**Core idea:** Source and target analogs are represented using simplices/simplicial complexes, and analogy is modeled through topological deformation.

**Relevance:** Very high to "concepts as simplices/complexes" and transformations of conceptual representations.

**Priority implication:** Any claim that simplicial complexes are being introduced as concept/knowledge representations must be heavily qualified.

## 2.2 Cavaliere, Senatore and Loia — semantic topological space

**Reference:** D. Cavaliere, S. Senatore, V. Loia, "Context-aware profiling of concepts from a semantic topological space," *Knowledge-Based Systems* 130, 102–115 (2017). DOI: **10.1016/j.knosys.2017.05.008**.

**Core idea:** Terms are vertices in a semantic topological space represented by a simplicial complex; concepts occur at multiple granularity/refinement levels.

**Relevance:** Very high to concept extraction, refinement, and simplicial representation.

**Priority implication:** A modern paper should distinguish its observation-map/homology/gluing theorems from existing multigranular simplicial concept representations.

## 2.3 van Ditmarsch, Goubault, Ledent and Rajsbaum — knowledge and simplicial complexes

**Reference:** H. van Ditmarsch, É. Goubault, J. Ledent, S. Rajsbaum, "Knowledge and Simplicial Complexes," in *Philosophy of Computing*, Philosophical Studies Series 143 (2022), pp. 1–50. DOI: **10.1007/978-3-030-75267-5_1**. Preprint: arXiv:2002.08863.

**Core idea:** Epistemic notions such as knowledge, belief, bisimulation, common knowledge and dynamics can be represented in simplicial-complex semantics.

**Relevance:** High to logical/cognitive interpretation of simplicial complexes and dynamics.

**Priority implication:** A new "Mental Space" theory should position itself relative to modern simplicial epistemic logic if it interprets simplices as information states.

## 2.4 David Spivak — simplicial databases

**Reference:** D. I. Spivak, "Simplicial Databases," arXiv:**0904.2012** (2009).

**Core idea:** Database schemas carry natural simplicial-set geometry; data-preserving maps form a category; limits and colimits model database operations such as joins/unions.

**Relevance:** High to concepts assembled from attribute relations and to categorical gluing.

**Priority implication:** The use of simplicial geometry plus limits/colimits to organize relational information has strong antecedents.

# 3. Object-attribute relations, Dowker complexes and Formal Concept Analysis

This is probably the most important adjacent mathematical literature for a rigorous concept theory.

## 3.1 C. H. Dowker — homology of relations

**Reference:** C. H. Dowker, "Homology Groups of Relations," *Annals of Mathematics* 56(1), 84–95 (1952). DOI: **10.2307/1969768**.

**Core idea:** A binary relation gives two dual simplicial complexes with corresponding homological information.

**Relevance:** Extremely high. If objects and attributes form a relation, Dowker complexes give canonical simplicial objects without inventing ad hoc logical simplices.

**Priority implication:** A paper that builds concept complexes from object-attribute incidence should test whether the construction is already a Dowker complex or a close variant.

## 3.2 Functorial Dowker theory

**Reference:** S. Chowdhury, F. Mémoli, "A functorial Dowker theorem and persistent homology of asymmetric networks," *Journal of Applied and Computational Topology* 2, 115–175 (2018). DOI: **10.1007/s41468-018-0020-6**.

**Core idea:** Functorial Dowker constructions and persistent homology for asymmetric relations/networks.

**Relevance:** Very high if unfolding/refinement is represented as a changing object-attribute relation.

**Priority implication:** This may offer a more canonical route to functorial concept complexes than hand-built simplicial maps.

## 3.3 Formal Concept Analysis

**Reference:** B. Ganter and R. Wille, *Formal Concept Analysis: Mathematical Foundations*, 2nd ed., Springer (2024). DOI: **10.1007/978-3-031-63422-2**. First English edition 1999.

**Core idea:** Formal contexts (object-attribute incidence) generate concept lattices via a Galois connection.

**Relevance:** Extremely high to "concepts made of attributes," hierarchical concept relations, closure, and reconstruction.

**Priority implication:** The thesis's category-of-concepts hierarchy should be compared with concept lattices before new claims are made.

## 3.4 Anton Ayzenberg — topology of nerves and formal concepts

**Reference:** A. Ayzenberg, "Topology of nerves and formal concepts," arXiv:**1911.05491** (2019).

**Core idea:** Connects Formal Concept Analysis, nerve constructions, homotopy topology and neural-code applications; the concept lattice is related homotopically to the nerve complex.

**Relevance:** Exceptionally close to the proposed bridge among concepts, nerves, hierarchy and topology.

**Priority implication:** This should be treated as a central antecedent in any theorem-level novelty analysis.

### Priority conclusion for this cluster

A likely strongest mathematical base for a new theory is:

\[
\text{observation relation}
\to
\text{FCA closure / Dowker complexes}
\to
\text{refinement maps}
\to
\text{homology / persistence}.
\]

This is cleaner than rebuilding concept hierarchy directly from propositional formulas.

# 4. Boolean and discrete differential calculus

## 4.1 Steinbach and Posthoff

**Reference:** B. Steinbach and C. Posthoff, *Boolean Differential Calculus*, Synthesis Lectures on Digital Circuits & Systems (2017), Springer/Synthesis. DOI: **10.1007/978-3-031-79892-4**.

**Core idea:** Systematic derivative operations for Boolean functions/lattices.

**Relevance:** Directly relevant to the thesis's distinguishing operator.

**Priority implication:** The identity

\[
\neg x_j\wedge \partial_j^B(x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge x_m)
\]

should be positioned as a structural identification with existing Boolean differentiation, not a new calculus.

## 4.2 Möbius / algebraic-normal-form transforms

Boolean functions on the subset lattice admit Möbius/Zhegalkin/Reed–Muller style transforms. These are mathematically adjacent because unfolding traverses Boolean-lattice ranks.

**Relevance:** Medium. They may help derive spectral or inversion formulas, but the thesis operator is not itself a Möbius transform.

### Priority conclusion

The strongest potentially original result here is not the derivative itself, but a theorem explaining how the thesis's **OR-valued gated derivative**, **Boolean-lattice rank operator**, and **characteristic-two cochain differential** are related by change of algebra.

# 5. Nerves, Čech constructions, gluing and reconstruction

## Standard antecedents

The following are classical and should not be presented as novel:

- nerve construction;
- Čech complexes;
- the nerve theorem under good-cover conditions;
- colimits/pushouts of subcomplexes;
- simplicial subdivision and homotopy invariance.

Useful standard references include:

- A. Hatcher, *Algebraic Topology* (2002);
- A. Björner, "Topological Methods," in *Handbook of Combinatorics* (1995);
- standard category/topos texts for colimits and descent.

### Priority implication

The publishable question is not whether faces can be glued in principle. It is:

> **Which observational data are sufficient for exact concept reconstruction, and what is the minimal extra structure when bare face data are insufficient?**

That can lead to a genuine classification theorem.

# 6. Sheaves, cosheaves and contextuality

## 6.1 Abramsky and Brandenburger

**Reference:** S. Abramsky and A. Brandenburger, "The Sheaf-Theoretic Structure of Non-Locality and Contextuality," *New Journal of Physics* 13 (2011), 113036. DOI: **10.1088/1367-2630/13/11/113036**.

**Core idea:** Measurement contexts form a cover; contextuality is characterized via obstruction to global sections of a sheaf-like event structure.

**Relevance:** High if "conscious differentiation" is formalized as context-dependent observation and compatible local data.

**Priority implication:** Any use of sheaf global-section obstruction must acknowledge this mature framework. A cognitive application would need genuinely new semantics or theorems.

## 6.2 Justin Curry — cellular sheaves and cosheaves

**Reference:** J. Curry, *Sheaves, Cosheaves and Applications*, PhD thesis / arXiv:**1303.3255** (2013).

**Core idea:** Computable sheaves/cosheaves on cell complexes, applications to TDA, networks, sensor systems, persistence and derived constructions.

**Relevance:** Very high to local-to-global concept data and to the distinction between restrictive (sheaf) and assembly-like (cosheaf) behavior.

### Priority conclusion

For the thesis's "gluing concepts" language, cosheaves may be at least as natural as sheaves. Sheaf descent should be used for compatible local descriptions; cosheaf colimits for assembling local contributions.

# 7. Persistent and zigzag homology

## 7.1 Foundational persistence

**Reference:** H. Edelsbrunner, D. Letscher, A. Zomorodian, "Topological Persistence and Simplification," *Discrete & Computational Geometry* 28(4), 511–533 (2002). DOI: **10.1007/s00454-002-2885-2**.

**Reference:** A. Zomorodian, G. Carlsson, "Computing Persistent Homology," *Discrete & Computational Geometry* 33(2), 249–274 (2005). DOI: **10.1007/s00454-004-1146-y**.

**Relevance:** Directly applicable if increasing observational resolution yields a filtration.

## 7.2 Zigzag persistence

**Reference:** G. Carlsson, V. de Silva, "Zigzag Persistence," *Foundations of Computational Mathematics* 10, 367–405 (2010). DOI: **10.1007/s10208-010-9066-0**.

**Relevance:** Extremely high because a psychologically plausible differentiation process may add, remove, split or reinterpret simplices rather than produce a monotone filtration.

### Priority implication

Persistence machinery itself is standard. Potential novelty lies in proving that a particular observation/refinement semantics yields a functorial persistence or zigzag module and in interpreting the resulting interval structure rigorously.

# 8. Topology in neural and cognitive modeling

These works show that topology is already a substantial tool in cognitive/neural science, though generally at the neural-data level rather than at Matte-Blanco's conceptual level.

## 8.1 Giusti, Pastalkova, Curto and Itskov

**Reference:** C. Giusti, E. Pastalkova, C. Curto, V. Itskov, "Clique topology reveals intrinsic geometric structure in neural correlations," *PNAS* 112(44), 13455–13460 (2015). DOI: **10.1073/pnas.1506407112**.

**Relevance:** Simplicial/clique topology of neural correlations.

## 8.2 Curto

**Reference:** C. Curto, "What can topology tell us about the neural code?" *Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society* 54(1), 63–78 (2017). DOI: **10.1090/bull/1554**.

**Relevance:** Overview of topological methods in neural coding.

## 8.3 Babichev, Cheng and Dabaghian

**Reference:** A. Babichev, S. Cheng, Y. A. Dabaghian, "Topological Schemas of Cognitive Maps and Spatial Learning," *Frontiers in Computational Neuroscience* 10:18 (2016). DOI: **10.3389/fncom.2016.00018**.

**Relevance:** Simplicial/topological schemas for cognitive maps.

### Priority implication

A concept-level unfolding paper should avoid general claims that topology is new to cognition. Its distinct target is a formal theory of **concept differentiation and reconstruction**, not neural topology in general.

# 9. Conceptual spaces

## 9.1 Peter Gärdenfors

**Reference:** P. Gärdenfors, *Conceptual Spaces: The Geometry of Thought*, MIT Press (2000). DOI: **10.7551/mitpress/2076.001.0001**.

**Core idea:** Concepts are represented geometrically using quality dimensions, regions, prototypes and similarity.

**Relevance:** High conceptual competitor/neighbor to "Mental Space," but the geometry is metric/convex rather than simplicial-homological.

## 9.2 Current development

**Reference:** P. Gärdenfors, "The Geometry and Dynamics of Meaning," *Topics in Cognitive Science* 17(1), 34–56 (2025). DOI: **10.1111/tops.12767**.

**Reference:** S. Scholz, "Conceptual spaces as predictive models," *Synthese* 207, article 237 (2026). DOI: **10.1007/s11229-026-05549-2**.

**Relevance:** These demonstrate continuing development of geometric concept representation through 2026.

### Priority implication

A simplicial Mental Space paper should explain why simplicial/incidence structure is needed in addition to metric conceptual spaces—for example, to represent genuinely higher-order compatibility, holes, descent, and non-pairwise relations.

# 12. Novelty window remaining after the search

The safest potentially publishable niche is **not** a claim that any one standard ingredient is new. It is a rigorously delimited reconstruction of the 2015 idea with new model-specific theorems.

A strong paper could make these contributions:

1. formally distinguish the historical Boolean unfolding from the chain differential;
2. prove the historical operator is representation-sensitive and classify the information needed to make it invariant;
3. construct an observation/refinement category whose complexes are generated canonically (preferably via nerves or Dowker complexes);
4. prove refinement maps are canonical on homology up to contiguity;
5. use mapping cones/relative homology to quantify structural changes;
6. prove exact reconstruction under explicit descent/provenance/filler hypotheses and prove impossibility without them;
7. separate homological features, automorphism symmetry and observational indistinguishability in one formal framework.

No source located in this search packaged exactly those seven items as a mathematical reconstruction of Matte-Blanco's unfolding function. That is the current opportunity.
