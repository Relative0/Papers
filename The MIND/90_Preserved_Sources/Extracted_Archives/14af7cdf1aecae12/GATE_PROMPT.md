# 1. Frozen starting point

Treat the following as the mathematical baseline to audit and extend, not as assumptions that may silently be strengthened.

Let \\(Q\\) be the current finite \\(T_0\\) observational poset and let a new Boolean observation \\(\psi\\) refine it.

For each \\(s\in Q\\), one or both refined states

\\[ (s,0),\qquad (s,1) \\]

may occur.

Write

\\[ Q^\psi\subseteq Q\times\\{0,1\\} \\]

for the actual refined carrier.

The previous gate introduced a **split completion**

\\[ \widehat Q^\psi \\]

which retains the old coarse comparisons between all available copies, while the actual refined order retains only comparisons whose new Boolean coordinate is nondecreasing.

Thus

\\[ Q^\psi=(\widehat Q^\psi)\_b, \qquad b(s,a)=a. \\]

The projection

\\[ \pi:\widehat Q^\psi\to Q \\]

is homotopy-neutral, and the carrier-splitting defect is represented by

\\[ \widetilde H_n\\!\left(\operatorname{Cone}(\Delta\pi);R\right) \cong H_n\\!\left( \Delta(\widehat Q^\psi), \Delta(Q^\psi);R \right). \\]

The corresponding relative chain complex is generated by chains

\\[ x_0<\cdots \<x_k \\]

in \\(\widehat Q^\psi\\) whose bit word

\\[ b(x_0)b(x_1)\cdots b(x_k) \\]

is **not nondecreasing**, i.e. contains at least one descent \\(1\to0\\).

Call this the **inversion complex** or **inversion-chain complex**.

The previous research gate also established a complete integral classification when the weighted height satisfies

\\[ h\_\psi(Q)\le 2. \\]

In that regime the defect is represented by a rooted incidence graph, the relevant matrix is totally unimodular, and acyclicity is equivalent to a rooted-forest condition.

The present problem begins **strictly beyond that regime**.

# 2. Critical negative constraint

Do not search for a universal higher-dimensional rooted-forest theorem.

The existing same-carrier one-bit thinning theory already indicates that:

- integral torsion can appear in higher dimension;
- unrestricted one-bit thinning can realize highly general finite-poset topology;
- coefficient-independent graph classification therefore cannot hold universally.

Carrier splitting contains same-carrier thinning as a boundary case, so any proposed universal theorem must confront these obstructions.

A successful result must therefore depend on a **genuinely restrictive but mathematically natural structural class**.

---

# 3. Central research question

Determine whether there exists a meaningful class \\(\mathcal C\\) of carrier-splitting systems for which the higher-dimensional inversion complex has an **exact combinatorial classification**.

By “exact classification” we mean substantially more than:

> the map is a homotopy equivalence if all Quillen fibers are contractible.

An exact result should ideally determine one or more of:

\\[ H\_\*(\widehat Q^\psi,Q^\psi;\mathbb Z), \\]

its Betti numbers,

its torsion,

its homotopy type,

its simple-homotopy type,

whether it is a wedge of spheres,

whether it collapses,

or precisely when the cone is acyclic,

from an explicit and checkable combinatorial invariant of the refinement.

The target is a theorem of the schematic form

\\[ \boxed{ (Q,\psi)\in\mathcal C \quad\Longrightarrow\quad H_i(\operatorname{Cone}U\_\psi;\mathbb Z) = F_i(\mathcal I(Q,\psi)) } \\]

for a concrete combinatorial object or invariant \\(\mathcal I(Q,\psi)\\).

Even better would be a necessary-and-sufficient classification.

# 4. Investigate at least four structural programs

Do not commit prematurely to one class. Compare several.

### Program A — Boolean-cube and Horn signature posets

Study observational posets that arise naturally as subsets of Boolean cubes

\\[ Q\subseteq\\{0,1\\}^m \\]

with coordinatewise order.

Subclasses to investigate include:

- full Boolean lattices;
- intervals of Boolean lattices;
- induced subposets of cubes;
- order-convex subsets;
- meet-closed subsets;
- join-closed subsets;
- distributive lattices;
- closure systems;
- models of Horn formulas;
- models of dual-Horn formulas;
- implication-closed signature systems;
- coordinate observations \\(\psi=x_j\\);
- observations defined by monotone Boolean functions.

Determine whether the inversion complex becomes:

- shellable;
- collapsible;
- Cohen–Macaulay;
- sequentially Cohen–Macaulay;
- a wedge of spheres;
- a matroidal/Boolean complex;
- or describable by intervals, antichains, minimal forbidden implications, or closure operators.

In particular, test whether **Horn closure** or distributivity removes the torsion phenomena seen in unrestricted thinning.

Do not assume that it does.

---

### Program B — Bounded comparability structure

Search for classifications based on bounded structural complexity of \\(Q\\), such as:

- bounded height;
- bounded width;
- cover graph a tree or forest;
- cover graph of bounded treewidth;
- comparability graph of bounded treewidth;
- series-parallel cover graphs;
- chordal comparability structures;
- bounded number of branching points;
- bounded dimension of the poset;
- bounded size of split fibers interacting along a maximal chain.

Determine whether some such hypothesis forces the inversion boundary matrices into a tractable family such as:

- network matrices;
- totally unimodular matrices;
- regular matroids;
- graphic/cographic constructions;
- block-triangular matrices;
- iterated mapping cones of graph incidence maps.

The goal is not merely an efficient algorithm.

Look for an exact structural theorem.

For example, can one characterize precisely when all integral homology is torsion-free under a bounded-comparability hypothesis?

Can the Betti numbers be obtained from a graph, hypergraph, tree decomposition, or Möbius invariant?

---

### Program C — Controlled lower-fiber or upper-fiber topology

The previous gate observed that lower-fiber obstructions concentrate at \\(0\\)-only coarse states and upper-fiber obstructions at \\(1\\)-only coarse states.

Develop this seriously.

For

\\[ L_s=\pi^{-1}(Q\_{\le s}), \\]

and the analogous upper fibers, investigate systems for which the exceptional fibers are:

- contractible;
- acyclic;
- spheres;
- wedges of spheres;
- shellable;
- Cohen–Macaulay;
- connected to a specified degree;
- or drawn from a finite family of homotopy types.

Ask whether a controlled collection of exceptional fibers yields an **exact decomposition of the inversion cone**, rather than only connectivity bounds.

Investigate whether the cone splits as a wedge, homotopy colimit, iterated cofiber, or direct sum in homology indexed by exceptional fibers.

Explicitly compare anything found with:

- Quillen's Theorem A;
- finite-poset fiber lemmas;
- Björner–Wachs–Welker type fiber theorems;
- mapping-cylinder constructions;
- homotopy-colimit decompositions.

A result counts as genuinely useful only if it computes more than these general theorems already provide.

---

### Program D — Descent filtration of the inversion complex

Exploit the structure peculiar to this problem.

An inversion simplex is not arbitrary: it is a chain carrying a binary word with at least one descent.

Filter the relative chain complex by one or more of:

\\[ \\#\\{\text{descents}\\}, \\]

the position of the first descent,

the position of the last descent,

the number of \\(1\\)-before-\\(0\\) inversions,

or the set of coarse states at which outcome availability changes.

Construct the resulting filtered complex and investigate its spectral sequence.

Determine whether the \\(E^1\\) or \\(E^2\\) page decomposes into standard interval/link/fiber homology.

A particularly valuable result would be a collapse theorem such as

\\[ E^2=E^\infty \\]

for one of the structural classes above.

Search for conditions under which the inversion complex decomposes into contributions associated to individual descents, with higher differentials measuring interactions between those descents.

This is potentially more intrinsic to the present theory than importing a generic nerve theorem.

# 11. Candidate theorem forms to attack

Attempt several theorem shapes.

Examples include, but are not limited to:

### A. Wedge theorem

For \\((Q,\psi)\in\mathcal C\\),

\\[ \operatorname{Cone}(U\_\psi) \simeq \bigvee_i S^{d_i}, \\]

with multiplicities determined combinatorially.

### B. Torsion-free classification

For \\((Q,\psi)\in\mathcal C\\),

\\[ H\_\*(\operatorname{Cone}U\_\psi;\mathbb Z) \\]

is free abelian and its ranks are given by an explicit invariant.

### C. Collapse criterion

\\[ \operatorname{Cone}(U\_\psi)\simeq \* \\]

if and only if an explicitly defined higher-dimensional inversion incidence object is collapsible/acyclic.

### D. Fiber decomposition theorem

\\[ \operatorname{Cone}(U\_\psi) \simeq \bigvee\_{s\in E} \Sigma^{k_s}F_s \\]

for explicitly specified exceptional fibers \\(F_s\\).

### E. Spectral-sequence collapse theorem

A descent filtration yields

\\[ E^1\_{p,q} = \bigoplus\_{\alpha\in I_p} \widetilde H_q(L\_\alpha) \\]

and collapses at \\(E^r\\) under a structural hypothesis.

### F. TU theorem

All boundary matrices of the inversion complex are totally unimodular if and only if the inversion data satisfy an explicit combinatorial condition.

These are research targets, not statements to presume true.

# 14. Success criteria for this gate

Classify the outcome into exactly one of the following levels.

**Level A — Strong success**

A meaningful higher-dimensional structural class admits a necessary-and-sufficient exact classification, with proof and computational verification.

**Level B — Significant success**

A natural class admits an explicit homology/homotopy formula or an exact collapse/TU/wedge criterion, even if not every invariant is classified.

**Level C — Useful partial success**

There are strong sufficient conditions, a spectral sequence, or decomposition theorem that materially improves generic fiber theory, but no closed classification.

**Level D — Negative but informative**

The investigated natural classes fail, with explicit minimal counterexamples and a clearer obstruction theory.

**Level E — No progress beyond standard theory**

The results reduce essentially to known nerve, fiber, or mapping-cylinder theorems.

Do not label Level C or below as a completed classification.
