# Recovered independent mathematical reconstruction
This reconstructs the mathematical argument recorded in the earlier review. No new first-reading independence is claimed for this recovery.

## 1. Disjoint balls
If B is an open radius-r ball, x,x'∈B and y∉B, recentering gives d(x,x')<r≤d(x,y). For a closed ball, d(x,x')≤r<d(x,y). Thus the inside distance is strictly below the cross-distance. The strong triangle inequality gives d(x',y)≤d(x,y); a strict inequality would imply d(x,y)≤max{d(x,x'),d(x',y)}<d(x,y), impossible. Vary y similarly inside another disjoint ball. Singleton cases are immediate. Hence cross-distances are representative-independent.

## 2. Collapse and universal properties
For distinct cells B,C define δ(B,C) as the cross-distance, with δ(B,B)=0. Three distinct representatives give its ultrametric inequality. Repeated cells give equality/zero. Pulling δ back by q gives d_P, zero within a cell and equal to d across cells. It is a pseudoultrametric bounded by d. Any pseudometric e≤d vanishing within every cell satisfies e≤d_P separately inside/across cells. Since d_P itself is admissible, it is greatest, not merely maximal.

Finite (P,δ) is discrete. For more than one cell, sufficiently small positive balls pull back to individual cells; the one-cell case is immediate. Thus d_P induces τ_P, the unions of cells. The quotient topology on P from (X,τ_P) is discrete. Topological indistinguishability is precisely shared-cell membership. A continuous map from (X,τ_P) to a T0 space must identify such points; otherwise a distinguishing target open set pulls back to a forbidden distinction. Its unique factor is continuous because P is discrete. This is the Kolmogorov quotient. The zero-distance metric identification of (X,d_P) gives exactly (P,δ).

Set maps factor iff cell-constant. For a cell-constant nonexpansive f from (X,d) into a metric Y, choose x∈B,y∈C to get d_Y(f(x),f(y))≤d(x,y)=δ(B,C). The factor is nonexpansive.

Counterexamples to altered contracts: an identity map between indiscrete two-point spaces is continuous but not constant, so T0 cannot be removed. From a discrete two-point latent space to itself the identity is continuous but does not collapse a one-cell partition, so the source cannot be replaced by (X,d).

Binary length-two strings with blocks {00,10},{01},{11} give collapsed distances d_P(10,01)=1 and d_P(10,00)+d_P(00,01)=1/2. Thus arbitrary partitions can fail even the ordinary triangle inequality. Ball geometry is sufficient, not necessary: equilateral finite spaces admit every partition.

## 3. Threshold towers and completion
For positive r, d≤r is transitive by the strong inequality. Classes are closed r-balls and are also open. Total boundedness gives a finite cover by smaller balls, each lying in a class, hence finitely many classes. Refining thresholds decreases equivalence and increases d_k and τ_k. The error is zero across cells and d≤r_k within cells, so 0≤d−d_k≤r_k. Every d-neighborhood contains a sufficiently fine cell. Therefore the topology GENERATED by the union of τ_k is the d-topology; the plain union need not be a topology.

The first distinguishing level κ exists, and the threshold convention gives r_κ<d(x,y)≤r_(κ−1).

Let X-hat be the completion, compact because X is totally bounded. At a fixed level, finitely many cell closures cover X-hat. Distinct cells have a positive constant cross-distance, which persists under limits. Closures are thus disjoint and positively separated, hence clopen. Compatible nested cells have diameters at most r_k→0. Representatives are Cauchy; their completion limit lies in every closure, uniquely. Conversely each completion point defines one compatible sequence. Coordinate maps are continuous; a continuous bijection from compact X-hat to the Hausdorff inverse limit is a homeomorphism.

For distinct compatible sequences, once their coordinates differ, all subsequent quotient distances equal that same cross-distance. Thus D=lim δ_k is well-defined, positive off the diagonal and satisfies the strong inequality. Limits of representatives show it is exactly the completion metric. Basic finite-coordinate neighborhoods contain an original cell, proving density of X.

Counterexample to recovering incomplete X: eventually-zero binary sequences have all finite prefixes, so the inverse limit contains every binary ray, including the missing all-ones ray.

## 4. Radius conventions
For d=λ^ell, with ell the number of common initial digits, a k-cylinder is a closed λ^k-ball and, for k≥1, an open λ^(k−1)-ball. An open λ^k-ball fixes k+1 digits. Level zero is the whole space. First distinction κ=ell+1 gives d=λ^(κ−1). This equality depends on calibration; arbitrary thresholds only give bounds. For m=p prime and λ=1/p, least-significant-digit-first expansions give Z_p and its residue ring quotients; Z_p is not Q_p.

## 5. Full-tree pointwise stabilizers and index
A finite complete frontier fixes a finite upper skeleton when fixed pointwise. Descendant sections are arbitrary and independent full rooted automorphisms, so G_F is the product of Aut(T_m) over s∈F. Each section is transitive on infinite rays: recursively select the next child permutation along two chosen rays and extend elsewhere. Hence the boundary orbits are exactly C_s.

For independent counting, take a finite height L exceeding all frontier depths. Kernel of restriction to L is contained in both stabilizers. Full rooted automorphisms are specified by one child permutation at each internal vertex. Fixing F imposes identity permutations above its leaves; all portraits below them remain free. Refining F to F' removes exactly N=|I(F')\I(F)| free permutation positions. Their group orders therefore differ by (m!)^N. Equivalently, split one current leaf at a time; each child-action kernel has index m!, and subgroup indices multiply without requiring normality of the final inclusion.

## 6. Exact normality iff
Let H=G_F'≤G_F. If H is normal then g(Hx)=H(gx); thus G_F permutes its boundary-orbit partition, the finer cylinders. Distinct vertices have distinct cylinders for m≥2. Fix an old s and a finer t below it at relative depth h. The full section at s is transitive on that entire level. Therefore every cylinder sΣ_m^h must occur as a finer orbit. They cover C_s; prefix freeness excludes any other relative depth. This proves necessity separately in each old cell.

Conversely, a full relative level h_s gives the kernel of restriction Aut(T_m)→Aut(T_(m,h_s)), an onto homomorphism. The product of these kernels is normal with quotient product_s Aut(T_(m,h_s)). At h_s=0 the finite tree is a single root and the quotient factor is trivial.

Alternative obstruction: in an uneven relative frontier, a shortest leaf u and an internal vertex v exist at the same depth. A full rooted automorphism can send u to v. A nontrivial child permutation supported below u fixes the frontier, but its conjugate below v moves frontier descendants. Hence nonnormality.

Concrete edge cases:
- F={0,1}; F'={00,01,100,101,110,111}: relative heights 1 and 2; index 2^4=16 and normal. Equal absolute or common relative depths are unnecessary.
- Ternary F={root}; F'={00,01,02,1,2}: two new internal vertices, index 6^2=36, nonnormal. A root swap conjugates a permitted permutation below leaf 1 to a forbidden one below internal vertex 0.
- Ternary uniform depth two: four new internal vertices, index 6^4=1296, normal.
- Simultaneously splitting a subset S of old cells once gives heights 1 on S and 0 elsewhere; quotient Sym(m)^|S|. Empty S is allowed.
- Binary F={root}, F'={00,01,1}: index four but nonnormal. Successive normal kernels do not make their composite inclusion normal.

## 7. Changed groups and nonregular trees
For setwise stabilization, the first-level frontier is preserved by every rooted automorphism, so its setwise stabilizer has index one, versus m! for pointwise fixation. The theorem cannot be reinterpreted setwise.

For the proper subgroup K fixing the first binary level, the uneven frontier {00,01,1} has stabilizer normal in K: it is a level-one kernel in the 0 factor and the whole 1 factor. The full-group necessity fails under this changed ambient contract. The trivial subgroup also immediately invalidates factorial indices and ray orbit claims. Finitary automorphisms are level-transitive but countable, so cannot be transitive on an uncountable boundary; finite-level transitivity alone is insufficient.

For a nonregular tree whose root has nonisomorphic binary and ternary descendant trees, the full group cannot swap these children. Splitting just the binary child gives a normal kernel although the frontier has mixed depths measured from the root. Local permutations preserve descendant-tree isomorphism types. More simply, d(a,b)=1/2, d(a,c)=d(b,c)=1 has full-isometry orbits {a,b},{c}, unlike its coarsest one-cell observation. Geometry alone does not produce every observation orbit.

## 8. Frontier appendix
Finite complete frontiers correspond bijectively to finite prefix-closed internal sets, including the empty set/root frontier. Refinement is inclusion. Union and intersection give join and meet; distributivity follows from sets. No finite top exists because any leaf can be split.

Edge counting gives m|I|=|I|+|F|−1, hence |F|=1+(m−1)|I|. Under uniform measure, disjoint covering gives Kraft sum one. Conversely, for a finite prefix-free family, a nonempty complement is open and contains a positive-mass cylinder, contradicting mass one. Finiteness matters: {0,10,110,...} has Kraft sum one yet misses the all-ones ray.

For finite distinct sample rays A, I_A consists of prefixes containing at least two samples. It is prefix-closed and contained in the finite union of pairwise common-prefix sets. Any separating frontier must split every vertex in I_A. Splitting exactly these vertices leaves at most one sample in each cell. This proves unique least refinement and minimum leaf count, not just one minimizer. For zero/one sample, I_A is empty and the root frontier is the answer. The feature order and allowed prefix tests are fixed.

## 9. Entropy versus index
Splitting mass w into child masses wp_a changes entropy by
−Σ wp_a log2(wp_a)+w log2 w = wH(p).
At w=0 every term is zero. Summing new internal vertices telescopes, independently of split order, giving H(F')−H(F). Uniform independent digits weight each split by m^(−|v|)log2 m. Logarithmic index instead contributes log2(m!) per split. The binary balanced frontier has entropy 2; the comb {000,001,01,1} has masses 1/8,1/8,1/4,1/2 and entropy 7/4. Both have three internal vertices and index eight. A point-mass source has zero entropy under every refinement but a strict refinement still has positive group index. No equality with information gain or consciousness follows.
