## Priority 1 — the only active mathematical target

### Exact question

Let P be a finite poset of height at most two and a:P->{0,1}^2. Let Q retain exactly the P-comparisons whose labels are coordinatewise ordered. If Delta(Q)->Delta(P) induces an isomorphism on F2 homology, must Delta(P) collapse directly to Delta(Q)?

Equivalent matrix form: must every invertible F2 relative deleted-edge/deleted-triangle matrix arising from a **two-bit** label admit a unique perfect matching?

### Why this is the highest-value question

One bit gives the proved rooted-incidence theorem. Three bits admit the independently verified eight-state counterexample with determinant one and three cyclic matchings. All two-bit profiles through five vertices passed the stronger direct-collapse property. Thus this is a sharply defined boundary between a complete theorem and a known failure, not an open-ended hunt for more examples.

The five-vertex evidence covers 337,920 profiles; it is evidence, not a conjecture proof. Exact conflict-coloring shows that the existing eight-state pair needs three bits, so simple relabelling cannot settle the question. The thirteen-state Z/2 torsion witness already uses two bits and disproves the rational-homology version: the F2 assumption is essential. The six-vertex two-bit attempt timed out and adds no coverage.

### Machinery and attack plan

Exploit the four-label diamond structure rather than treating B as an arbitrary matrix. Classify the possible label patterns on deleted triangles, their signed supports, and how alternating cycles can propagate through transitive coarse orders. An invertible matrix with multiple perfect matchings is the exact obstruction. Compare the resulting support restrictions with total balance, sign-nonsingularity, and discrete-Morse dependency graphs, crediting existing matrix theory rather than renaming it.

For counterexample search, encode transitivity, height<=2, two-bit labels, square relative cell counts, invertibility, and an alternating cycle. Use symmetry reduction on label classes and poset isomorphisms. Export any candidate as an explicit relation list and integer boundary matrix; validate it with the independent checker. A computational search result without a compact certificate is insufficient.

### Falsification strategy

Try to compress the known three-bit eight-state example into four labels without changing its critical deleted incidences. Separately construct cyclic matching supports allowed by two-bit triangle types, then ask whether those supports are realizable by a transitive poset. This separates the algebraic obstruction from the realization obstruction.

### Stopping criterion

Stop when there is either (a) a complete structural proof, or (b) an explicit verified counterexample and an explanation of the mechanism. If neither is reached within a fixed, predeclared search/theorem-development budget, freeze the statement as a conjecture with the existing n<=5 evidence. Do not automatically increase vertex bounds indefinitely. The article can still be written around the proved one-bit theorem and the three-bit limit.

### Destination

Preferred Paper A sharpness section; if the result is sufficiently substantial, it supplies the missing central theorem for conditional Paper B.

## Priority 2 — certification and minimization, after Priority 1 stops

### Exact question

Can the key counterexamples be given short, independent certificates with reduced observation counts, and which minimality claims can actually be proved?

### Why it matters

The 13-state Z/2 block has now been compressed to exactly two bits, and the eight-state noncollapse pair provably needs three. The 79-state Z/3 labels are not optimized. Minimum **vertex counts** for the relevant relative pairs remain unresolved. Smaller or better-certified examples improve exposition; vague claims of “smallest” would hurt credibility.

### Machinery

Exact Smith normal forms, conflict-graph coloring for label minimization, finite-poset symmetries, and explicit spanning-tree/regular-CW constructions. R15's absolute torsion lower bound applies to the tree-target route, not automatically every relative pair.

### Falsification and stopping

Test the claimed minimum only within an explicitly stated family. Stop once the existing examples have a second independently written certificate checker and, at most, one successful simplification. Do not pursue global minimum-state torsion across all relative poset pairs unless a separate structural bound appears. This is a finite quality-improvement task, not a new paper by itself.

### Destination

Supplement or short examples section of Paper A/B.

## Priority 3 — conditional dimension problem, not active in parallel

### Exact question

For Q contained in a **specified total coarse order** P, characterize the conflict graphs on deleted comparisons. Is this graph class algorithmically tractable for coloring, or does it realize a known hard class?

### Why it matters

It is the exact unresolved restriction left behind by the false v0.5 2-dimension equality. The corrected general problem is already a generalized Boolean rectangle-cover problem and is NP-complete at height one. A nontrivial special-family theorem is what could justify a dimension paper.

### Machinery

Generalized Boolean rank, directed separation demands, interval/overlap representations, graph coloring and perfect-graph criteria, and exact poset transitivity constraints. Do not assume the conflict graph is an interval graph, Ferrers graph, or perfect graph; each needs proof or a counterexample.

### Falsification and stopping

Enumerate small target suborders of a fixed chain and seek forbidden induced subgraphs of candidate tractable classes. Any proposed equivalence must be checked against both two-vertex counterexamples to classical dim2. Stop if the family is identified with existing literature without a new useful corollary; document the established name and keep this as a technical note. Activate only after Priority 1 is resolved or frozen.

### Destination

Conditional Paper C; otherwise corrected technical note.
