## Lauro-Grotto: correction target and fairness

Freshly inspected evidence: the full text hosted on the author's ResearchGate publication page, identified there as author-uploaded content, plus the indexed institutional PDF text and the University of Florence repository's open-access record. The source links and failed image-access attempts are recorded in `SEARCH_AND_ACCESS_LOG.md`.

The article links cluster membership to indistinguishability on p.537 and discusses symmetry on pp.538-539. Page 539 also invokes a distant-object perceptual analogy. Paper U should credit that analogy instead of implying that an observation restriction is wholly absent from the motivating literature. The article's metric/ultrametric contrast is not a theorem that ultrametrics induce an indiscrete topology. Do not attribute that particular false theorem to Lauro-Grotto; the manuscript properly assigns the explicit indiscreteness error to the author's thesis.

The mathematical correction itself is sound: in a three-point ultrametric with d(a,b)=1/2 and d(a,c)=d(b,c)=1, a and b remain distinct and separable. Equal distances from c only prevent distinction by that particular external-distance observation. Even positive-distance equilateral metric points have distinct metric neighborhoods. An observation map makes the intended restriction explicit. This reviewer analysis is independent of whether the psychological motivation is accepted.

Fresh article page images were not obtained: the institutional site returned an anti-bot challenge, ResearchGate's live browser showed a device check, and publisher/DOI requests failed. No challenge was solved or bypassed. This review does not repeat the earlier package's claim of fresh visual inspection as its own. The accessible author-posted text suffices for the modest paraphrase and fairness repair, but an image-level critical edition check of that article is not claimed.

For the second paragraph of the interpretation section, a concise replacement is:

```latex
Matte Blanco's discussion includes treatment of class members and
parts as identical \cite[p.~39]{MatteBlanco1975}; he also distinguishes
this treatment from the external observer's description of parts and
wholes \cite[pp.~148--149]{MatteBlanco1975}.
Our quotient is a deliberate restriction: $x$ and $y$ remain different
latent points, and $q(x)$ is a class label, not an assertion that a

infinite sets, and conscious unfolding

consequences of this construction. The observable algebra is Boolean,
no part--whole bijection is inferred, and a resolution index is not a
```
