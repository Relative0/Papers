## 2. The findings that changed the manuscript

### The combined architecture has close predecessors

The draft correctly acknowledged that concepts-as-balls were old, but the stronger overlap matters. Khrennikov's 2007 section 4 explicitly uses fixed-prefix equivalence classes as associations. Khrennikov and Yurova's 2017 sections 4 and 9 combine an observational quotient with p-adic/ultrametric latent states. Kleiner's 2020 section 3.4 connects accessible equivalence classes with structural automorphisms. These sources defeat a claim that the basic architecture is new merely because its pieces have been joined under cognitive terminology. Full references and access grades are in the priority map, C05, C13 and C14.

### The core topology is sound, but standard

The regular-prefix metric, ball conventions, observation topology, collapsing ultrapseudometric and inverse-limit description survive. Partition topology and metric identification already have established names and theory. The revision unifies their universal properties without presenting several descriptions of one quotient as several independent inventions.

The main v0.1 orbit clause is **not** erroneous under its explicitly defined full regular-tree group. The needed repair is to prove transitivity explicitly and prevent the clause from being generalized to nonregular trees or arbitrary subgroups.

### Adaptive refinement yields an index, not always a quotient group

The strongest mathematical sharpening is now Theorem 1.1. For finite complete frontiers F<=F' in the full regular m-ary tree, if N is the number of newly split vertices, then

    [G_F : G_F'] = (m!)^N.

But G_F' is normal in G_F exactly when each old frontier cylinder is refined to one full relative level. The level can differ between old cylinders. In the normal case, the quotient is a product of finite rooted-tree automorphism groups.

The binary frontier {00,01,1} gives an index-four nonnormal subgroup of the full group. Thus a quotient-group interpretation of an arbitrary adaptive refinement would be wrong. The proof is complete under the full regular-tree contract. It uses standard group-theoretic mechanisms; first priority for the exact packaged criterion is not established.

### Total boundedness and compactness cannot be interchanged

A calibrated threshold tower on a totally bounded ultrametric space recovers its **completion**. It recovers the original space when that space is compact/complete. The eventual-zero binary sequences demonstrate why the distinction matters. Similarly, arbitrary thresholds locate a distance between successive radii; the exact formula in terms of first distinguishing depth requires the prefix metric's particular scale calibration.

### The Matte-Blanco attribution needed substantive correction

The source's stronger identity/part-whole language cannot fairly be reduced to a claim about equivalence without identity. The revised model is expressly a weaker class-relative observational construction. It does not implement relation-converse symmetry, removal of negation, literal part/whole identity, or conscious unfolding. Exact primary printed-page locators are supplied, with an explicit OCR/source-collation limitation.

## 3. What is actually stronger after revision?

The note now has an explicit coordinate-free finite-ball quotient theorem, rather than relying entirely on digit notation. It identifies the topology and metric quotient, represents all allowed observables, and characterizes the collapse as the greatest dominated pseudometric vanishing within cells. It states exactly why the ball geometry matters: arbitrary partitions can fail the triangle inequality when cross-distances are preserved.

The adaptive index/normality theorem adds a genuine mathematical distinction absent from the draft's short adaptive discussion. It also prevents an attractive but false interpretation. A companion entropy calculation shows why a finite symmetry index does not measure information gain: the binary balanced and comb frontiers both have index eight but entropies 2 and 7/4 bits under the uniform measure.

These are useful strengthened statements and explanations. They are not, on the evidence obtained, a certified new research contribution. A specialist could reasonably regard the adaptive criterion as a standard corollary worth stating clearly in a synthesis note.

**2. **4. What does ultrametricity add beyond an arbitrary partition?** Transitive threshold equivalence, a compatible hierarchy of balls, constant cross-distances and a canonical distance-preserving collapse. Observational equivalence alone does not require it.

**5. What do the p-adics add beyond an arbitrary rooted tree?** Canonical congruence classes, compatible residue-ring maps and arithmetic. Those features are precise but not needed for the central results. Hence the p-adics remain a short specialization.

It gives an exact orbit identification and measurable group-theoretic refinement index, with a real obstruction to quotient groups. The seven requested themes have been separated and precise primary page locators supplied. Independent page-image collation is still needed before claiming a fully source-collated historical audit.

**10. Best publication form and likely venue family?** A short synthesis/correction note in mathematical modeling foundations, mathematical exposition, or mathematics-informed psychoanalytic theory. Not a pure-mathematics research submission based on an alleged new general quotient theory. No journal-specific acceptance forecast is made.
