# Boolean-Cube, Horn, and Distributive-Lattice Cases

## 1. Pure Horn refined signatures are homotopy-neutral

A Boolean relation is Horn exactly when its model set is closed under coordinatewise conjunction (intersection/meet).  Suppose the **realized refined signature set**

\[
Q^\psi\subseteq\{0,1\}^{m+1}
\]

is nonempty and meet-closed.

The meet of all its elements is a least element of `Q^psi`.  Coordinate projection commutes with meet, so

\[
Q=\operatorname{pr}(Q^\psi)\subseteq\{0,1\}^m
\]

is also meet-closed and has a least element.  Thus both order complexes are cones.  Any map between nonempty contractible spaces is a homotopy equivalence, hence the carrier-splitting cone is contractible.

The dual statement holds for join-closed / dual-Horn realized relations.

### Computational check

`verify_boolean_horn_cases.py` exhaustively enumerated all nonempty Boolean relations through four coordinates.  At four coordinates it found 4,959 meet-closed relations and 4,959 join-closed relations.  Every meet-closed relation and its projection had a least element; every join-closed relation and its projection had a greatest element.

### Scope warning

This does **not** say that every observation given by a Horn formula on an arbitrary old signature poset is neutral.  It says that if the entire **refined realized relation** is itself closed under meet, then the carrier and its projection are contractible.  The companion paper's Horn repair theorem is more flexible and therefore needs semilattice/cofinality hypotheses.

## 2. Boolean lattices give canonical high-dimensional defects

Let the coarse poset be the Boolean lattice `B_m`, let its bottom be 1-only, its top 0-only, and split every other state.  The exclusive inversion graph consists of the single endpoint edge.

The proper part of `B_m` has order complex `S^{m-2}`, so the matching theorem gives

\[
H_m(Cone(Delta pi);Z)\cong Z
\]

and zero reduced homology in all other degrees.

Direct Smith-normal-form checks were run for `B_2` and `B_3`, giving respectively

\[
H_2\cong Z,\qquad H_3\cong Z.
\]

The theorem predicts `H_4 ~= Z` for `B_4`.

This provides the requested genuinely dimension-greater-than-two examples without leaving a very natural observation poset.

## 3. Finite distributive lattices: sphere or zero

Every finite distributive lattice is `J(R)`, the lattice of order ideals of a finite poset `R`.

Under an exclusive-inversion matching, a bounded coarse poset can contribute only through the bottom-to-top inversion.  Thus the endpoint profile reduces to the topology of the proper part of `J(R)`.

Using the crosscut theorem:

- if `R` has a nonminimal element, the join of all atoms of `J(R)` is below the top; the atom crosscut is a full simplex, so the proper part is contractible;
- if every element of `R` is minimal, `R` is an antichain, `J(R)=B_r`, and the atom crosscut is the boundary of an `(r-1)`-simplex.

Hence

\[
H_n(Cone(Delta pi);Z)=
\begin{cases}
Z,&J(R)=B_r\text{ and }n=r,\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\]

for the endpoint matching profile.

The verifier checked all order-ideal lattices generated by naturally labelled posets with at most three generators (10 lattices), with no failures.

## 4. Interpretation

The Boolean/distributive program therefore has two sharply different subregimes:

- **closure-system regime:** full Horn or dual-Horn closure forces a cone and kills the defect;
- **exclusive-endpoint regime:** Boolean lattices produce a single high-dimensional spherical defect, while non-Boolean distributive lattices are neutral.

This is mathematically cleaner than a broad claim that "Horn structure removes torsion."  It does not: the closure-system theorem is contractibility, and the matching theorem can still transfer arbitrary torsion once one leaves that closure regime.
