1000: \begin{theorem}[Block-lift theorem]
1001: If
1002: \[
1003:  f(a,b)=g(a)\,\Theta\,h(b),
1004: \]
1005: then
1006: \begin{align}
1007:  [f]_{A\mid B}&=\cO_\Theta(t_g,t_h),
1008:  \label{eq:blocklift}\\
1009:  [M_f(\mathbf X)]_{A\mid B}&=\cO_{\widetilde\Theta}\bigl(\ell_g(\mathbf X_A),\ell_h(\mathbf X_B)\bigr).
1010:  \label{eq:symbolicblocklift}
1011: \end{align}
1012: Valuation commutes with both constructions.
1013: \end{theorem}
1014: \begin{proof}
1015: At the entry indexed by $(a,b)$, the numeric right side is $g(a)\Theta h(b)=f(a,b)$.  The formula-valued statement is the same identity after polarity substitution, and valuation preserves $\Theta$.
1016: \end{proof}
1017: 
1018: \begin{theorem}[Rank and conjunctive/XOR separability]
1019: For a fixed bipartition $A\mid B$:
1020: \begin{enumerate}
1021: \item $f(a,b)=g(a)\land h(b)$ for some Boolean functions $g,h$ if and only if
1022: \[
1023:  \operatorname{rank}_{\F_2}[f]_{A\mid B}\le 1.
1024: \]
1025: For a nonzero function the factorization is the ordinary rank-one factorization
1026: \begin{equation}
1027:  [f]_{A\mid B}=t_g\,t_h^T,
1028:  \label{eq:separable}
1029: \end{equation}
1030: and over $\F_2$ the nonzero factors are unique.
1031: \item More generally, the minimum $k$ for which
1032: \begin{equation}
1033:  f(a,b)=\bigxor_{i=1}^{k} g_i(a)\land h_i(b)
1034:  \label{eq:xorrankdecomp}
1035: \end{equation}
1036: equals $\operatorname{rank}_{\F_2}[f]_{A\mid B}$.
1037: \end{enumerate}
1038: The formula-valued flattening of a rank-one term factorizes analogously as the outer product of the two polarity vectors.
1039: \end{theorem}
1040: \begin{proof}
1041: A conjunctive factor $g(a)\land h(b)$ has matrix $t_g t_h^T$ and therefore rank at most one.  Conversely, every nonzero rank-one matrix over $\F_2$ is $uv^T$ for nonzero Boolean vectors $u,v$, which are the truth vectors of unique Boolean functions on the two blocks; the zero matrix is obtained by taking one factor identically zero.  For the second statement, every summand in~\eqref{eq:xorrankdecomp} has rank at most one, so $k$ is at least the matrix rank.  A rank factorization supplies exactly that many rank-one outer products, proving equality.
1042: \end{proof}
1043: 
1044: This gives a precise modern form of the separability/factorization idea already present in the original LM manuscript \cite{Droncheff2018}.  The statement concerns ordinary $\F_2$ rank and XOR decomposition, not the different OR-based notion usually called Boolean rank.  The value also depends on the chosen bipartition: repartitioning the variables can change the flattening rank.
1045:

1047: Let
1048: \[
1049:  g(W,X)=W\xor X,\qquad
1050:  h(Y,Z)=\neg Y\land Z,\qquad
1051:  f=g\Rightarrow h.
1052: \]
1053: In true-first order $(11,10,01,00)$,
1054: \[
1055:  t_g=(0,1,1,0),\qquad t_h=(0,0,1,0).
1056: \]

1058: \begin{equation}
1059:  [f]_{(W,X)\mid(Y,Z)}=
1060:  \begin{bmatrix}
1061:  1&1&1&1\\
1062:  0&0&1&0\\
1063:  0&0&1&0\\
1064:  1&1&1&1
1065:  \end{bmatrix}.
1066:  \label{eq:fourbyfour}
1067: \end{equation}
