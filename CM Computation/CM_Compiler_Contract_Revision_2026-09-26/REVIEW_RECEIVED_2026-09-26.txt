## 2. The most concrete new issue is the dense-output contract

The manuscript describes dense output over **requested unfixed axes**. The inspected implementation instead retains **all axes in the supplied ambient row and column layouts**, including axes whose variables have been fixed or become irrelevant.

For example, take

\[
e=(X\land Y)\lor Z,
\qquad F=\{Z\mapsto0\},
\]

with row axes `[X,Z]` and column axes `[Y]`.

The remaining function is \(X\land Y\), which has a four-entry truth table over its two unfixed variables. But the dense implementation reinserts the retained \(Z\) axis by broadcasting and returns a

\[
4\times2
\]

array: **eight entries, not four**. This follows directly from the dense wrapper and `lift_cm`.  

**This is not an error in the Boolean answers. It is an error in how the manuscript describes the returned object and its size.**

The least disruptive repair is to document the existing behavior accurately: distinguish the retained ambient layout from the unfixed or essential variables, and use the retained-axis count for allocation and materialization costs.

A projected-output API would also be valid, but that would be a code change requiring new tests and appropriately versioned measurements.

## 3. The formal rules need to separate valid derivations from selected compiler behavior

The current inference system permits more than one provenance derivation for some expressions.

For instance, \(X\land Y\) can receive an **S** derivation through primitive recognition, but the displayed TAB rule also permits a **T** derivation. Both produce the same correct token.

That is harmless for semantic soundness. It becomes problematic when the judgment is also presented as though it uniquely describes what a particular compiler strategy does.

A second example makes the distinction clearer:

\[
e=X\lor(Y\land Y).
\]

The tabulation rule is applicable to this two-variable expression. Nevertheless, the **pure-structural strategy** can fail because it is not allowed to use that rule. Thus “no pair judgment is derivable” is not an accurate unqualified description of failure under every selected strategy. The implementation explicitly distinguishes these strategies. 

The repair is straightforward:

> Keep the existing judgments as a relation describing admissible derivations, and separately specify the ordered, strategy-dependent compiler procedure that selects an outcome.

The paper should also define its support calculation explicitly as **syntactic variable occurrence after excluding fixed names**, rather than leaving readers to assume essential functional dependence. The implementation collects the former. 

These are important specification repairs, but they do **not** require abandoning the soundness proofs.

## 4. Successful child folds are not necessarily retained after root failure

The source confirms a significant operational boundary:

> If root pair compilation fails, the dense wrapper passes the **original expression** to the ordinary builder.

It does not retain a rewritten tree containing successfully compiled pair children. 

Consequently, a diagnostic counter showing successful child pair operations does not necessarily show optimizations that survived into the returned result. Some work was attempted and then discarded.

This matters for both exposition and benchmarking. The paper should distinguish **attempted/local pair constructions** from **committed contributions to the returned compilation**. The ordinary builder may perform its own optimizations; the finding is specifically that the pair wrapper does not preserve its successful child results on root failure.

This should be documented and tested rather than silently “fixed” by implementing a different persistent local-rewriting algorithm.

## 5. Novelty remains narrower than the mathematical presentation might initially suggest

The current manuscript deserves credit for already acknowledging that the pointwise algebra is classical.

The fresh literature check confirmed a particularly direct antecedent: **Cheng, Zhao, and Xu’s Proposition 3.3 gives the same-variable truth-vector identity underlying entrywise outer Boolean combination**. Their paper also defines XOR–AND Boolean matrix multiplication. The revised manuscript’s acknowledgment of that antecedent is appropriate. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="9"}

The low-level implementation primitives also have close practical counterparts. Kitty’s official interface includes truth-table Boolean operations, variable swaps and flips, dependence tests, cofactors, and base expansion. These operations should not carry the paper’s novelty burden. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="10"}

For stronger circuit-optimization positioning, the comparison becomes more demanding: DAG-aware AIG rewriting already combines local truth functions, input/output transformations, sharing-aware decisions, and a distinction between candidate and committed rewrites. That is not the identical algorithm, but it is close enough to require a precise comparison. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="11"}

**My conclusion is not that nothing publishable remains.** It is that the strongest defensible contribution is closer to:

> An explicit, frame-carrying specification of a restricted truth-function compiler, with strategy provenance, correctness guarantees, fallback and output boundaries, and an honest account of when the mechanism does or does not provide an implementation benefit.

That can be useful. But the paper still needs to show why this integration offers a reusable research contribution rather than simply careful documentation of familiar operations.

The most promising strengthening would be an executable contract and failure-mode suite, paired with a directly matched comparative study—not unrelated additional theory.

## 6. The empirical story is honest, but not complete for this compiler

The manuscript correctly distinguishes three different things:

**Exploratory mechanism evidence.** The reported S1/S2 results show useful reductions relative to direct expansion, but the matched generic local optimizer agrees with CM on the principal symbolic-output metrics. That supports a local optimization mechanism, not a uniquely CM-specific advantage.

**Negative dispatcher evidence.** The reported P14-PY0 result is a legitimate negative boundary on recipe-to-owned-ANF compilation. Its aggregate exact-dispatcher/direct ratios are approximately 1.0508 and 1.0607, with lower ratios better. Selected favorable examples do not overturn that aggregate result.

**The designated pair-compiler confirmation.** The current manuscript expressly says that protocol v3 remains unexecuted. I did not establish an executed campaign matching that primary endpoint from the available package.

These distinctions are documented in the audit’s empirical section with exact manuscript locations. I checked their interpretation and internal consistency, but did not regenerate the historical statistics from raw data.

### Does the confirmatory experiment have to be completed before publication?

**Not for every possible paper identity—but it is necessary for a demonstrated-performance identity.**

A good methods or negative-results paper does not need a positive speedup. However, merely relabeling the current paper “methods” does not eliminate the need for a substantial contribution and appropriate evidence.

My recommendation is to complete **one focused, task-matched study** using the already declared primary pair endpoint and direct packed comparator. A valid negative or parity result can still support the narrowed paper. Changing endpoints after an unfavorable result would be much less defensible.

A small natural-input admission survey would also be valuable: how often does the current root-only pair path actually apply, and how much apparent child-level opportunity is discarded at root failure?
