\section{Executive determination}\label{executive-determination}

\textbf{Decision: conditional GO as a focused mathematical methods note;
not an unqualified GO as a substantial new-theory research article.} The
revised manuscript is mathematically coherent under its explicit
contracts, self-contained where it needs to be, and accompanied by
runnable evidence. The largest remaining uncertainty is contribution
size and theorem-level priority, not a known surviving proof error.

The supplied package already contained a September 24 adversarial report
and a September 26 research-viability assessment. However, the first
primarily attacks the immediately preceding zero-aware/Walsh-centered
version, and its copies are duplicates rather than additional
independent reviews. The second was a selected-proof viability
assessment, not certification by an external panel. The current
restricted-dispatch revision therefore needed its own review.

This report records five specialist passes and two explicitly hostile
passes conducted by the same assistant. Their separation helps expose
different failure modes, but does not create independent expert
testimony. No outside mathematician was recruited, no journal referee
was contacted, and no formal proof assistant was used. Literature
searches and bounded computations are evidence, not guarantees of
correctness or originality.

The deliverable is a genuine revision of the supplied Paper B, not a
replacement by the separate CM/LM foundations paper or the exact-query
paper appearing in the user's example scorecard. The old 18-page
manuscript becomes the 14-page \textbf{Guarded Boolean Operators: Exact
Dispatch and a Polynomial Guard-Degree Tradeoff}. Its best result is now
the exact degree-versus-number-of-tests calculation for one complete
nonlinear example.

\section{2. Specialist review passes}\label{specialist-review-passes}

\subsection{2.1 Mathematical analysis and real algebraic
geometry}\label{mathematical-analysis-and-real-algebraic-geometry}

\textbf{Attack:} Rebuild the four-region classification, examine
connectedness rather than infer it from a picture, and try to defeat the
affine and polynomial lower bounds using extra guard components,
arbitrary coefficients, or nonconstant signatures inside a fiber.

\textbf{Finding:} The original score algebra is sound, but connectedness
deserves a direct proof. The substitution \(u=1/a\), \(v=1/b\) converts
all relevant score signs to affine signs. In the positive reciprocal
quadrant the four regions are intersections of open half-planes. Thus
they are convex there and connected in the original coordinates. This
proves connectedness and also limits the meaning of the claimed
nonlinearity.

The stronger exact guard-degree result survives a boundary-factor
reconstruction. The three score-zero arcs are associated with pairwise
nonassociate primitive irreducible quadratics. A finite sign library
must vanish along every switching arc somewhere among its defining
polynomials. Rational parametrization forces one polynomial to vanish
identically along an arc, so the corresponding quadratic divides it.
This establishes the lower bound over arbitrary real coefficients, not
merely an enumerated candidate library.

For two tests, the four labels exhaust all four binary signatures.
Therefore each label has exactly one signature, even though the general
definition permits signatures to vary within a label. Each bit differs
from the AND-center bit on exactly two leaves. Its polynomial must
consequently contain two quadratic factors. Both tests therefore have
degree at least four. Two explicit quartics attain the bound.

\textbf{Disposition:} Accept with the stronger proof incorporated as
Lemma 3.1 and Theorem 3.2. The universal assertion is proved
analytically, not inferred from finite tests. The reciprocal-coordinate
limitation is stated beside the result.

\subsection{2.2 Formal semantics and
certification}\label{formal-semantics-and-certification}

\textbf{Attack:} Test vacuous guards, reachable all-zero cells, boundary
inputs, incomplete dictionaries, and solver uncertainty. Ask whether
three binary tests actually certify a total output.

\textbf{Finding:} Two real contract gaps required correction. First,
every universal sign assertion is vacuously true on an empty guard. The
statement that such a guard carries a unique semantically determined
operator therefore needs a nonemptiness assumption. Second, the
predicates \(h_i>0\) identify operators only on a promised zero-free
domain. At \((a,b)=(1,1/2)\) the four scores are \((2,0,-1,-1)\), but
all three binary predicates are false, just as for AND. A decoder that
silently treats the zero as a negative sign is not an exact strict-sign
dispatcher.

The revised certificate propositions require nonempty guards and
explicit zero-free coverage. Reduced observation uses a previously
certified complete dictionary and a zero-free promise. Reachability, a
genuine zero, mixed signs, and an analysis returning UNKNOWN are kept
distinct. The reference implementation uses exact trichotomy and returns
BOUNDARY rather than inventing a Boolean value.

\textbf{Disposition:} Accept after repair. No general
certificate-producing nonlinear solver is claimed. Exact rational
evaluation certifies a point; uniform signs over a whole guard are
established here by the paper's analytic proofs, not by sampling.

\subsection{2.3 Discrete algorithms and observation
complexity}\label{discrete-algorithms-and-observation-complexity}

\textbf{Attack:} Try two tests instead of three, query adaptively, allow
predicates other than truth-table coordinates, use target-specific
certificates, or hide feature computation inside one nominal test.

\textbf{Finding:} The original three-probe conclusion was defensible
only with the correct quantifiers. It is the unique minimum fixed
truth-table-coordinate basis, and three is also the deterministic
adaptive worst case because the AND transcript leaves an unqueried leaf
possible. A leaf, however, has a one-coordinate positive certificate. An
early-stop order has depths \(1,2,3,3\), so its uniform expected query
count is \(9/4\), not three.

Allowing arbitrary polynomial observations changes the contract: two
quartic signs encode all four labels. These are not two queries to the
original coordinate oracle. The general finite selection problem is the
classical differently-labelled-pair hitting formulation of minimum test
set, with established antecedents {[}P1, P2{]}. A weighted fixed basis
does not automatically optimize shared arithmetic, expected path length,
or adaptive depth.

\textbf{Disposition:} Accept with all four notions separated: fixed
predicate basis, fixed coordinate basis, adaptive coordinate
identification, and target-specific certification. No new generic
combinatorial invariant or solver algorithm is claimed.

\subsection{2.4 Boolean representation and CM/LM
algebra}\label{boolean-representation-and-cmlm-algebra}

\textbf{Attack:} Remove the unpublished companion citation and ask
whether the claimed lift remains defined, faithful, and compatible with
arbitrary pointwise Boolean connectives. Try a constrained physical
predicate frame instead of free Boolean generators.

\textbf{Finding:} A short self-contained proof is available. Define the
lift by minterm substitution into independent formal Boolean generators.
All-true evaluation recovers the truth table, proving injectivity.
Boolean evaluation commutes with pointwise connective application,
proving superposition. The same parameter-side distinction problem
therefore persists for truth tables, numeric CMs, and fixed-frame
symbolic LMs.

The free-frame hypothesis matters: all-true valuation is available for
formal generators, not necessarily for physically constrained
predicates. The paper now says so. Representation invariance concerns
exact distinctions under the same observation contract; it does not
imply equal output construction cost or a computational speedup.

The historical LM check was a literal self-comparison,
\texttt{truth{[}alpha\_idx{]}\ ==\ truth{[}alpha\_idx{]}}. Its PASS
message did not test an implementation of the lift. The fresh suite
evaluates minterms, signed substitutions, all valuations, and
superposition instead.

\textbf{Disposition:} Accept with direct Proposition 5.2 and a worked
projection example. CM/LM notation remains an optional endpoint, not the
source of the guard lower bound.

\section{3. Two hostile passes}\label{two-hostile-passes}

\subsection{3.1 Hostile mathematical referee: ``Your contracts are doing
hidden
work''}\label{hostile-mathematical-referee-your-contracts-are-doing-hidden-work}

This pass attempted to break claims with empty sets, a boundary
masquerading as AND, a singleton dictionary containing an excluded zero
input, an omitted coordinate, constrained LM variables, tangencies,
oscillatory crossings, and extra polynomial zero curves.

The first two attacks succeeded against overbroad formulations and
prompted explicit repairs. The omitted-coordinate attack demonstrates
the star lower bound rather than contradicting it. A singleton operator
family can have a zero-size distinguishing set only after totality on
the domain has already been established. The revised example with scores
\(1\) and \(t^2\) shows why this promise cannot be dropped.

A continuous score that merely takes both signs near zero need not have
a single well-defined crossing; fixed signs on the two punctured sides
are now required. A polynomial zero event can be an even-multiplicity
touch and need not change an operator. Trajectory intervals are
relatively open in \([0,T]\), so endpoint-containing pieces are allowed.

The strongest attempted attack was to make the two polynomial tests vary
inside a chamber. It fails because four labels require all four
signatures, leaving no spare signature for a label to use. This finite
coding observation closes a genuine possible gap in the stronger degree
theorem. Additional zero components likewise do not avoid the necessary
irreducible boundary factors.

\textbf{Final recommendation:} Mathematical acceptance under the
repaired and explicitly limited contracts. This is not a formal proof
certificate; a further independent human referee may still find an
oversight.

\subsection{3.2 Hostile handling editor: ``Where is the research
contribution?''}\label{hostile-handling-editor-where-is-the-research-contribution}

The exact quotient is essentially the partition into fibers of a
finite-valued map. The minimum-test formulation is standard. Walsh
geometry is classical. A numeric CM is a truth table in matrix form, and
the needed LM facts follow from substitution. None of these become new
theorems merely because the manuscript gives them new notation.

The nonlinear model is also less intrinsically nonlinear than its
appearance suggests: reciprocals turn its three boundaries into lines. A
reviewer could reasonably regard the exact degree tradeoff as a small
illustrative exercise using standard divisibility, polynomial
descriptions {[}P8{]}, and four-codeword combinatorics. Positive
coefficient rescaling adds no independent structural difficulty. The
existence of two quartic observations does not establish reduced
arithmetic cost, robustness, or practical superiority.

These criticisms were not dismissed. The title, abstract, opening
claims, theorem placement, appendix role, and conclusion now present a
focused methods note. The new contribution is the self-contained
complete worked analysis of three distinct observation contracts and
their certification requirements, including an exact degree threshold.
It is not asserted to replace DSGRN, establish a new abstract
interpretation theory, or solve general guard synthesis.

\textbf{Final recommendation:} Conditional consideration as a
methods/expository note. A venue expecting a substantial original
structural theorem, a new general algorithm, or an application with
demonstrated impact could still reject it for insufficient contribution.
The revision reduces overclaiming; it does not eliminate that legitimate
editorial judgment.

\section{4. Proof ledger for the revised
manuscript}\label{proof-ledger-for-the-revised-manuscript}

All 12 numbered theorem/lemma/proposition statements were reconstructed.
Remark 2.3 is additional, separately checked algebra. The unnumbered
finite-selection equivalence and information bounds were also checked.

\textbf{Proposition 2.1, exact quotient.} Finite strict-sign
intersections give open fibers; complementary fibers make them clopen in
the zero-free domain. Local path-connectedness of open Euclidean sets
justifies open components. A finite number of fibers need not imply
finitely many continuous-score chambers. The polynomial semialgebraic
finiteness statement is attributed to sign-condition theory {[}P6{]}.

\textbf{Theorem 2.2, four chambers.} The sums \(h_1+h_2=-2ab\),
\(h_1+h_3=-2b\), and \(h_2+h_3=-2a\) prohibit two positive entries. Four
interior witnesses establish realizability. Reciprocal normalization
proves connectedness and the three-leaf star adjacency. \textbf{Remark
2.3} checks \(Ax+By+Cxy=(AB/C)(x'+y'+x'y')\) with \(x'=Cx/B\),
\(y'=Cy/A\) for positive coefficients.

\textbf{Lemma 3.1, boundary factors.} Coprime coefficient pairs make
each linear-in-\(b\) quadratic primitive and irreducible over the
polynomial ring via Gauss's lemma. Rational parametrization and
finiteness of zeros force a factor for each covered arc. Constant tests
are explicitly discarded. The boundary argument uses nearby promised
inputs, not a value assigned on the boundary.

\textbf{Theorem 3.2, exact tradeoff.} Degrees at most one cannot cover a
quadratic arc. Degrees two or three can cover at most one distinct arc
per polynomial. Two quartics give the full four-label code. Any two-test
solution makes each polynomial carry two quadratic factors. No bounded
enumeration is used to infer this universal minimum.

\textbf{Propositions 4.1 and 4.2, certificates.} Nonemptiness prevents
vacuous uniqueness. A complete dictionary and certified zero-free domain
justify unobserved signs. Guard coverage is explicit. No existence of a
general effective certifier for arbitrary continuous inputs is asserted.

\textbf{Proposition 4.3, collecting semantics.} A reachable cell has an
everywhere-defined constant strict output exactly for the singleton sign
sets \(\{-1\}\) and \(\{+1\}\). Unreachable cells require a completion
choice; zero and mixed signs remain distinct.

\textbf{Proposition 5.1, coordinate costs.} Each AND/leaf pair forces
its unique differing coordinate. The all-zero AND transcript supplies
the adaptive adversary. Each leaf has a one-coordinate target-specific
certificate. This does not lower-bound arbitrary predicates in the
magnitude parameters.

\textbf{Proposition 5.2, LM faithfulness.} All-true evaluation in a free
Boolean frame returns the numeric table. Substitution commutes with
every pointwise Boolean connective. Therefore the semantic equality
classes agree across the representations under the same observation
model.

\textbf{Proposition A.1, crossings.} Fixed signs on punctured sides, or
a simple zero with the other scores nonzero, imply exactly one changed
entry. Oscillation and simultaneous zeros are excluded by hypotheses.

\textbf{Proposition A.2, trajectories.} A closed discrete subset of a
compact interval is finite; complementary intervals are relative
intervals. The polynomial root bound counts discriminant events, not
guaranteed switches. An identically zero polynomial is excluded from
that bound.

\textbf{Proposition A.3, Walsh calibration.} The simplex Gram identity
identifies the score image with the zero-sum subspace. Explicit
coefficients realize every nonconstant sign pattern. Regular facet
witnesses prove Hamming-one adjacency for \(n\ge2\). The
coincident-hyperplane unary case is stated separately. Classical
antecedents are credited rather than treated as cleared novelty.

\section{5. The strengthened result and its
limits}\label{the-strengthened-result-and-its-limits}

For the real model \(F(x,y)=x+y+xy\), put

\[h_1=a-b-ab,\qquad h_2=-a+b-ab,\qquad h_3=-a-b+ab.\]

Let \(m_d\) be the smallest number of fixed binary tests of the form
\(H(a,b)>0\), each with total polynomial degree at most \(d\), whose
joint signature identifies the induced operator on the entire zero-free
positive quadrant. The revised theorem is

\[m_1=\infty,\qquad m_2=m_3=3,\qquad m_d=2\quad(d\ge4).\]

The two tests can be \(h_1h_3<0\) and \(h_2h_3<0\). They encode AND,
\(X\), \(Y\), XNOR as \(00,10,01,11\). Each polynomial in every exact
two-test solution must have degree at least four. The same statement
holds after positive linear coefficient rescaling, but not unchanged
under arbitrary coordinate changes.

This is stronger than merely saying that affine guards fail and three
score probes work. It establishes a complete threshold for this
degree-constrained library model. It does \textbf{not} establish a
general polynomial dispatch complexity theory, a new asymptotic lower
bound, or a runtime advantage. When boundary rejection is included
without a promise, the implementation uses ternary product signs; those
are not the binary outputs counted by \(m_d\).

\section{7. Prior-art and priority
determination}\label{prior-art-and-priority-determination}

The review used primary papers, author-hosted manuscripts, publisher
records, and arXiv records. It checked current 2026 entries instead of
assuming that a pre-cutoff bibliography was sufficient. The search
ledger records concrete queries, source locations, comparisons, and
access limitations. No finite search establishes that no antecedent
exists.

\textbf{Minimum test set is not new.} Moret and Shapiro's original
formulation {[}P1{]} explicitly minimizes tests without losing
discrimination. Berman, DasGupta, and Kao {[}P2{]} give the
differently-paired-object set-cover formulation and approximation
results. The manuscript's finite optimization is an instance, not a new
algorithm or generic invariant.

\textbf{Continuous-to-logical parameter organization is not new.} DSGRN
{[}P4{]} builds a computable finite parameter decomposition carrying
substantially richer dynamical information. Its regulatory-network
hypotheses differ from this note's arbitrary finite scores. The
comparison should neither deny the close conceptual precedent nor
falsely identify the two mathematical settings. The 2026 Boolean/DSGRN
preprint {[}P5{]} is relevant current context, not evidence that the
present bilinear degree calculation has been duplicated.

\textbf{Global separation is not per-target specification.} Anthony's
2026 threshold-function paper {[}P7{]} develops target-specific
specification numbers and chamber geometry. The revised manuscript
explicitly distinguishes its single fixed separating basis from those
quantifiers and from adaptive identification.

\textbf{Polynomial descriptions are an important adjacent antecedent.}
Averkov and Brocker {[}P8{]} study minimum polynomial-inequality
descriptions of polyhedra and special semialgebraic sets. Their
set-description question is not automatically the same as simultaneous
four-label decoding with a fixed degree cap and promise domain.
Nevertheless, it makes a sweeping claim that fewer high-degree
polynomial tests are a new general phenomenon untenable.

\textbf{The remaining novelty uncertainty is narrow but real.} This
search did not locate the exact \(m_d\) statement for this exact
signed-magnitude example. That absence is insufficient to certify
priority. The elementary proof could be known, implicit in broader
description theory, or simply too small to carry a research article. The
revised paper therefore presents the calculation and synthesis
accurately without a first-ever claim.
